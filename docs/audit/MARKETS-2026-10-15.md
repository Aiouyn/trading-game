# Market realism and player experience audit

**Scope:** stocks, major and meme crypto, stablecoins, commodity proxies, bond funds, listed options, market clock, and visible tape. **Revision:** `be21a34852d2244ea56c824ef1e85042ea7aecf7`. No production code or saves changed. The audit includes an isolated analysis script and result for the stablecoin probe.

## Verdict and scores

**Market realism: 4/10. Market fit for the intended immersive fortune-building game: 6/10.** The engine has useful market scaffolding: deterministic replay, shared price impact, regimes, GARCH volatility, jumps, event shocks, rate exposure, and a broad instrument catalog. The largest immersion gap is that a full-mode close auction changes the marked price once per game day while the visible retail tape consists of synthetic, price-neutral prints. That weakens a player's ability to understand why prices moved. Some abstractions—commodity proxies, bond funds, and European-style cash-settled options—are suitable choices for an accessible game and should not be scored as failures merely because they omit deliverability, a full bond ledger, or American exercise.

## Highest-severity findings

### P1 — Full-mode close auction concentrates a disproportionate share of price variance

`advance` marks a day boundary at `tick % ticksPerDay`; `ecologyStep` returns early on other ticks. On the final tick it runs all ecology pools and, in full mode, sets the asset close to the ecology price. That makes the closing auction one return among 720 but a large discontinuity for the player’s chart, position marks, stops, and options.

The supplied `proof/economy-audit/experiments.json` used three seeds and 30 days (64,800 ticks per symbol, with ordinary and close returns separated). Closing returns account for **36.43% of HLCN squared tick returns**, **33.42% for AUC**, and **22.85% for FROG** despite being 1/720 of ticks. These are descriptive results from 3 seeds × 30 days, not a universal calibration. They establish concentration at the close, not that those returns are universally too large. The design issue is observability: a daily auction may be intentional, but the chart/tape needs to identify its prices and causes so the player can distinguish it from ordinary intraday movement.

**Change priority:** choose and surface a clear market clock: either make the ecology auction a visible close/open event with a causal auction imbalance and auction prints, or distribute its flow through the day while retaining day-level clearing. Don’t smooth it merely to improve a statistic; preserve causal accounting and show the price/flow causes.

**Acceptance proposal (not a market-derived threshold):** in staged checks, first replay the existing 3-seed × 30-day sample and verify price/cause/tape reconciliation for every closing auction; then expand to at least 20 seeds × 60 days across liquid stock, major crypto, meme, and stablecoin examples to estimate whether the observed close concentration is stable. Select any variance-share threshold only after the product defines how visible/large an auction should be. If the close can move marks materially, the UI should label the auction and expose its causes regardless of the eventual threshold.

### P1 — Simulated retail trades create a convincing-looking but non-economic tape

The `retail.ts` module documents that crowd prints do not move prices or variance. `retailTick` creates 10 crypto, 6 stock, or 3 other prints per symbol, splits sides from a hash, gives each a small notional, and prices them inside the capped spread. Experiment evidence confirms **1,080 generated prints and 995 distinct displayed names**, while market prices and accounts remain unchanged. The crowd tape is therefore visualization, not liquidity, orders, or trading agents. Existing named rivals are separate, slower-clock flow, as stated in the same module docs.

This is an understandable performance shortcut, but the player experience reads as thousands of traders participating. If these prints are used to infer volume, activity or sentiment, the inference is false by construction. A neutral display-only ticker can be fine if unmistakably labeled; it cannot substantiate realistic volume, depth, or player-versus-market behavior.

**Acceptance:** public volume equals settled fills plus explicitly labeled simulated pool volume; a display-only print never contributes to volume, VWAP, candles, sentiment, or execution claims. If retail is promoted to economic agents, their orders must have cash/inventory constraints, submit signed flow, clear against price-setting liquidity, and conserve cash plus asset supply.

### P1 — Spread caps erase much of the intended liquidity difference in the riskiest assets

The base market data gives meme coins 80–110 bps configured spreads and low ADV values (`cryptoDef` overrides for FROG/MOON/HAMR in `universe.ts`). `halfSpread` applies a volatility-scaled half spread capped at 15 bps per side, or 30 bps round trip (`trading.ts`). The experiment confirms FROG's configured 90 bps becomes **30 bps effective**; a $1,000 trade costs about 16.4 bps, while $1m moves the mid 0.215% and costs about 58.1 bps. Impact is not capped. This is a gameplay ceiling with real trade impact retained, though it compresses spread differences in stressed meme names.

**Acceptance proposal:** decide whether the cap is an intentional player-protection rule and make the effective cap visible in quotes. A bounded execution probe should confirm that marginal impact rises with order size and that displayed bid/ask match fills at the shown spread; the existing experiment already provides a first size ladder for HLCN, AUC, and FROG. No statistical tier threshold is proposed here.

### P2 — 5-second Binance data is a sanity check, not a matching calibration set

The supplied spot history is labeled as two UTC days per asset, one-second kline closes resampled into five-second closes (`proof/economy-audit/historical-spot.json`). Binance's official repository documents the archive source. The pooled PEPE sample contains 34,558 returns and has **0.1669449% absolute-return P99**; pooled BTC P99 is 0.0660029%. These are real-time centralized-spot samples, while game ticks use a compressed economic clock and distinct asset populations. They are reference points only, not matched calibration. In particular, do not compare HLCN to BTC or FROG to PEPE as if they were identical instruments or horizons.

The Cont–Kukanov–Stoikov study finds short-interval price changes relate to order-flow imbalance and market depth, with traded volume a noisier proxy. The game's square-root impact/depth framing has a defensible stylized basis, but a single signed net-flow auction and exogenous “ADV” are not an order book or calibrated depth process. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.6402) [Binance public data](https://github.com/binance/binance-public-data).

**Acceptance proposal:** calibrate by instrument population and matched horizon, then publish quantiles, flat interval frequency, jump frequency, volatility clustering and execution shortfall with sample windows and hashes. Expand source dates before making any realism claim; the current two-day sample is a narrow reference only.

## Instrument-specific realism and playability

**Stocks.** `advance` combines market/sector/region factors, GARCH volatility, jumps, earnings catalysts and price pull toward value; `stockDef` assigns dividends, circuit breakers, shares outstanding, spread and ADV. That is a solid stylized foundation for a game. In full ecology, pool flow then sets the closing price. Listed assets without a company supply account trade against a Street counterparty, and human limit orders are not matched against each other. Those are legitimate abstractions for a solo simulation, but the player should not be led to believe they represent conserved market-wide supply or a live exchange book.

**Major crypto, memes and stablecoins.** `cryptoDef` uses a common crypto factor and always-open price path; meme overrides give individual names different volatility, jumps, drift, spread and ADV. This supports distinct game personalities. AUSD and FLUX have low volatility and downside jumps, but the implementation's mean-reversion half-life only pulls price toward the moving `lnV`; `advance` continues to evolve `lnV` through permanent diffusion and jumps. It is not a hard $1 anchor. I ran a bounded test-only probe on 20 seeds: after applying a +25% fundamental shock to isolated AUSD/FLUX and simulating 30 game days at 24 ticks/day in full ecology, median terminal `lnV` remained +25.22% / +25.93% from $1, while median spot was +1.87% / +2.20%. This shows that the displayed spot can stay much closer to $1 while the modeled fundamental remains depegged; it does **not** establish depeg behavior under a realistic reserve run, because the probe shock is artificial and there are no reserves/redemptions. Reproduce from `scripts/research/market-audit-stablecoin-probe.ts`; result is `proof/economy-audit/stablecoin-anchor-probe.json`.

**Commodity proxies.** The catalog gives gold, oil, natural gas, wheat, coffee, power and other themed assets seasonal drift and sector exposure (`commodityDef` and commodity rows in `universe.ts`). As named speculative proxies in an immersive game, this is useful breadth; absence of futures expiry, storage, delivery and curve mechanics is an acceptable scope choice. Label them as proxies if the UI could otherwise imply that players own deliverable commodities. There is no player-experience finding here that those mechanics must be added.

**Bond funds.** Five funds map to 2/10/30-year rate tenors and credit risk; `advance` applies daily rate shocks. Duration differentiates AV2Y from AV30 and makes a useful rates-exposure loop. These are presented as funds, so they do not need individual bond issuance or delivery. The remaining product question is whether “pays the 2-year yield” promises a cash distribution or simply describes exposure; I did not verify a player-facing distribution contract in this audit.

**Options.** `optionQuote` prices options with Black–Scholes, game-day expiry, current regime vol adjusted by the underlying's GARCH `h`, and a minimum/proportional spread. Volatility is dynamic; the missing feature is a strike/tenor volatility surface with skew/smile. Contracts are long-only and cash-settled at expiry. That is a sensible European-style simplification for the game; American early exercise and a full dealer market are not required unless the product promises those strategies. The quote/payout relationship remains an important game-balance contract. Existing tests cover put-call parity and expiry, but the audit did not independently revalidate their full suite.

**Clock and sessions.** `advance` performs regime and rates updates at game-day boundaries; `ecologyStep` runs its auction at the close. `advance` applies a U-shaped volume multiplier over each game day and a constant multiplier for crypto. That is an intentionally compressed universal clock, not evidence of a realistic stock exchange calendar. Crypto's continuous schedule fits; the model does not encode separate stock/bond/commodity market hours in this path. With the probe's 720 ticks/day and 5 seconds/tick, one game day spans 3,600 seconds of wall time if the nominal cadence holds (24× compressed). This can work for an immersive game if the player-facing calendar and all daily finance events agree on that clock.

## What is working

- Deterministic seeded streams make market paths reproducible and testable (`sim.ts:131-132`, asset-specific RNGs).
- Volatility is regime- and GARCH-dependent; jumps and corporate catalysts create recognizable event risk (`sim.ts:181-186`, `:260-286`).
- Large orders do move price through impact, and account-specific temporary impact plus shared permanent impact make self-impact legible (`trading.ts:93-99`, `:1096-1109`). This is preferable to claiming that unlimited orders fill at a static quote.
- Different asset classes have meaningful starting parameters, including stablecoin peg risk, meme negative drift, commodity seasonality, and bond duration/rate exposure (`universe.ts:241-350`).
- Long option positions have a clear premium-at-risk and cash-settled expiry; the European-style contract is a suitable game simplification.
- Event/regime and rate layers have explicit boundaries, so game drama can be tuned without pretending the world event is a real macro policy change.

## Priority sequence

1. Reconcile price-setting close flow with what the player sees and with the intended daily clock; add close auction cause/prints to charts and volume.
2. Decide whether retail is economic or display-only. Label it accordingly, and never count synthetic prints as executed or price-forming volume.
3. Decide whether the 30 bps round-trip spread cap is an intentional accessibility rule; show effective spreads and preserve visible size-dependent impact.
4. Keep accurate product labels (“commodity proxies,” “bond funds,” “European-style options”) and clarify promised bond distributions.
5. Build a reproducible calibration suite by class and horizon, report uncertainty across seeds and dates, and avoid direct five-second-to-game-tick equivalence.

## Evidence and limits

**Regression assessment:** the captured suite reports 735/746 passing. The eight squeeze-test failures follow from the run producing no liquidation/squeeze trigger: the first assertions fail, then checks that dereference the missing event fail as well. This does not isolate whether the fixture became stale or the squeeze mechanism regressed. Two huge-money failures are unexpected `insufficient cash` rejects despite the test sizing orders from live free cash. They fail at the reject-count assertion, so those runs do not provide evidence for the later finite-value or balance assertions. Execution impact/mid-move making the affordability estimate stale is a plausible hypothesis, not a diagnosis from this audit. The remaining failure is newest-feed ordering and outside this market sub-audit.
