## What the charts show

The best available young-pool comparison is three actual Solana pools with verified creation timestamps and at least about 37 minutes of observed one-minute OHLCV: LIQUIFY (about 41 minutes old), Roadster Starman (about 93 minutes), and Mindforge/FORGE (about 167 minutes) at the source-data capture. They differ substantially in liquidity, venue, volume and data completeness, so they are examples rather than a representative sample. LIQUIFY falls sharply after its initial rise and becomes thin; Roadster has a base followed by several accelerating upward steps and a retrace; FORGE has early bursts, a long quiet/down interval, then a second surge and retrace. FORGE’s queried history has 40 missing minute intervals, preserved as gaps rather than padded bars. LIQUIFY has four missing intervals. Roadster has none. Raw OHLCV, timestamps, pool metadata and calculations are in [young-pool-api-evidence.json](../../proof/memecoin-visual-20261015/young-pool-api-evidence.json).

At the same labeled one-minute interval, the eight game launches show sharp bursts and retraces, but BEFORE the sampled candles almost never carry forward unchanged: flat OHLC bars range from 0% to 1.1%, with longest runs of zero to one minute. In the authoritative FINAL FREEZE snapshot, flat bars range from 6.3% to 14.9%, with longest runs of two to four minutes. Median absolute close change remains in a broadly comparable band (4.0%–7.2% BEFORE; 3.5%–8.0% FINAL FREEZE), as does median candle range/open (9.4%–12.5% versus 9.6%–13.1%). The quiet-only 8792 and initial risk-exit 8794 snapshots are retained as intermediate stages, not final. The final captures show the selected tokens still have burst-and-retrace patterns with short carried intervals; this does not make the game reproduce LIQUIFY’s collapse, Roadster’s staircase rally, or FORGE’s long lull and second pump. Those are distinct paths, not a single calibration target.

## Capture design and limits

The game evidence uses a disposable, sanitized preview DB, not the user’s original save. The current-code fixture issued eight NPC launch calls at five nominal runtime-minute intervals and stepped the actual simulation and fill settlement for 90 minutes after the youngest launch. It is a controlled launch-path sample, not evidence about natural launch frequency. At the source snapshot, tick 2220, the cohort ranged from 90 to 125 elapsed 1× runtime minutes (1.50–2.08 in-game days, since 720 ticks form one game day). BEFORE used world 8791; worlds 8792 and 8794 are intermediate stages; authoritative FINAL FREEZE uses world 8795. These previews share the seed and launch schedule. Screenshots were captured later while previews ran at 1×, so the UI’s rounded displayed age can be slightly higher than the snapshot age. Candle duration is nominal elapsed time at 1×; the chart axis follows the 390-minute game/session clock rather than wall-clock chronology. The frozen source hash for `src/world/launchpad/microflow.ts` is recorded in the manifest.

The source change gates ambient pit flow during scripted launch episodes and permits one bounded risk-triggered sale decision in a scripted no-fill minute when existing take-profit, stop or panic criteria fire; otherwise it takes no action. There are no startup or periodic market orders. The measured flat-bar result is a one-seed paired snapshot comparison. It is evidence of changed inactivity/counterflow in this fixture, not a broad distributional validation or proof of improved outcomes across all real launches. Harness-derived activity/return summaries were excluded because the observer did not capture the relevant NPC/script fills reliably.

The young real sample was selected from currently discovered pools, not randomly. It mixes PumpSwap and Pump.fun, and liquidity ranges from roughly $2.9k to $63.6k where reported; FORGE liquidity was unavailable. GeckoTerminal’s queried OHLCV window is bounded, and absent bars are not evidence of zero trades. These public pools may contain wash trading or concentrated holders. Their chart shape is descriptive evidence, not a claim that each is a healthy or desirable market. The four 6–7-minute pools in the gallery provide only opening microstructure; their one-minute views are too short to assess a 30–90-minute trajectory. Five older, established pools (ages shown as one to three years) provide mature context only. Their multi-hour one-minute windows are not matched-age comparisons.

All screenshots are visual captures of the chart applications themselves. Real-pool screenshots came from GeckoTerminal; game screenshots came from the maximized Launchpad preview at the same viewport. Prices and market states can change while pages are captured. Do not interpret these selected examples as statistically representative or as universal price calibration.

## Practical reading

The BEFORE issue visible in this sample was not simply too little volatility: large active-bar movement was already present. Nearly uninterrupted minute-to-minute activity made the chart cadence look mechanically busy compared with real pools that can go quiet, move in bursts, or remain inactive between waves. The frozen source adds short carry-forward windows while retaining broadly similar median active-bar size. That is a meaningful rhythm improvement, though two-to-four-minute final flat runs remain far shorter than some real-pool lulls, and scripted launch envelopes still constrain the broad trajectory. The visual evidence supports testing longer and variable quiet periods and stronger differentiation among launch outcomes; it does not by itself establish which parameters are right.
