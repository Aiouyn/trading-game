## What works

The new funded population is a real improvement over per-coin pretend wallets: `population()` opens 240 engine accounts and funds them once, and `buyCurve()` transfers cash from an account when a wallet has an `accountId` ([microflow.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/microflow.ts#L25), [trade.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/trade.ts#L80)). Sales credit the same account, and the accounts are retained through graduation. A single trader can therefore spend on several coins without receiving a fresh per-coin balance. This is the right accounting foundation for world traders.

Price delivery on the live curve also has useful constraints. Normal NPC buys and sells run through the same curve settlement and fee logic as player trades; account-backed orders cannot spend more than their cash or sell more than their holdings ([trade.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/trade.ts#L66), [microflow.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/microflow.ts#L99)). Historical order sizes are reserve-relative, but the game settles them on current reserves instead of copying the historical price path ([microflow.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/microflow.ts#L129)). The observed trader cash therefore does not get silently minted by this microflow.

There is a useful distinction between deterministic text and deterministic market movement: player replies are stored text only, while NPC replies are template lines with hash-based selection and do not perturb market random streams ([reply.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/reply.ts#L34), [reply.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/reply.ts#L61)). That is stable for replay, but it currently means conversation is cosmetic rather than a signal channel.

## Material failures and mismatches

**Paid promotion guarantees immediate outside-funded demand.** A board item costs $500 and adds 0.5 buzz; a Wire promotion costs $2,000 and adds 1.5 buzz. `DEMAND_PER_BUZZ` is $1,000, and `arrive()` converts buzz directly to pending dollar demand subject to the curve-to-graduation cap ([flow.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/flow.ts#L43), [demand.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/demand.ts#L54), [constants.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/constants.ts#L132)). The caller immediately absorbs the demand. If that path has no funded wallet, `buyCurve()` records the spend as an `npcRetail` faucet ([absorb.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/absorb.ts#L12), [trade.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/trade.ts#L84)). The independent experiment found the $500 board move instantly created $500 NPC cash and lifted price 5.859%; the $2,000 wire move created $1,500 NPC cash and lifted price 18.078%, each in one trade and before another player action. The wire demand is below its fee; the realism issue is that the paid action deterministically creates both the signal and its buyer, without uncertain reach, delayed conversion, or organic rejection.

**NPC “arrival” paths still bypass own balances.** The historical microflow is account-funded, but it is not the only NPC path. Legacy launch buzz, viral shocks, paid promos, crowd scalp/FOMO/news buys, and the launch sniper call `buyCurve()` without a wallet; those fills faucet cash. The corresponding legacy sells sink proceeds rather than returning them to a persistent owner ([flow.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/flow.ts#L77), [flow.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/flow.ts#L171), [crowd.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/crowd.ts#L355), [trade.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/trade.ts#L84), [trade.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/trade.ts#L182), [launch.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/launch.ts#L174)). So “persistent funded traders” accurately describes one layer, but not total NPC pressure or all named crowd behavior. `npcRetail` aggregates funding, sinks, and some fee flows; it should not be read as a count of separate solvent participants.

**Recent historical flow is a movement bootstrap, not yet a reactive behavioral model.** The flow library has 96 selected ten-minute historical episodes. At token setup, the code selects an episode by coarse affinity class, shuffles the 240 accounts, assigns 24, and maps each historical actor slot onto one of those accounts ([microflow.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/microflow.ts#L37), [microflow.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/microflow.ts#L64), [docs/MEMECOIN-HISTORICAL-FLOW.md](../MEMECOIN-HISTORICAL-FLOW.md)). These time/sign/size patterns are replayed without regard to current player chat, reply content, creator credibility, or token-specific news. A missing historical seller can be replaced by another actual holder, which avoids naked short inventory but changes the historical intent and actor ([microflow.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/microflow.ts#L139)). The discretionary overlay has five fixed styles selected by `id % 5`; its mood, panic, momentum, and activity are token-level shared state. It has no trader-level learned trust, accumulated loss memory, independent attention choice, or cross-token portfolio choice ([microflow.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/microflow.ts#L22), [microflow.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/microflow.ts#L163)).

This is a reasonable way to avoid dead-flat charts while orders are settled honestly. It is not evidence that NPCs understand a token or react credibly to a player. The parent-run controlled probe demonstrates the limitation directly: an accepted player reply and an accepted world-chat message each leave prices, accounts, orders, and flows identical to control for 120 ticks ([experiments.json](../../proof/economy-audit/experiments.json)). A reply is visible but inert. A chat post is not a market event.

**Headline sentiment is discarded for the launch crowd.** `applyNews()` recognizes themed earnings and other headline kinds but never reads `wire.tone`; each qualifying headline lifts story and schedules a positive `newsLeft` spend ([crowd.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/crowd.ts#L262)). In the independent paired probe, positive and negative earnings headlines produced identical full crowd state: both left $260 queued to buy with identical timing. Crashes/halts do create a scare, but ordinary bad earnings do not. This is a clear semantic defect, not merely a calibration preference.

**Realism comes from volatility quantity more than event causality.** The arrival path buys a shock in the same tick it is generated; each arrival is capped only at the remaining cash to graduation ([demand.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/demand.ts#L14), [absorb.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/absorb.ts#L12)). Viral shocks use a front-loaded heavy-tail hazard (1.43/day at launch, 0.35-day decay constant) and promo buzz has near-immediate half-life ([constants.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/constants.ts#L145), [constants.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/constants.ts#L73)). Those choices produce visible pumps, but their precise step-function timing and high-impact prints can feel arbitrary. Real young coins can move violently, yet a convincing simulation needs heterogeneous discovery, attention, trader conviction, wallet overlap, and exits to explain why this coin moves now. Large absolute tick changes alone do not provide that explanation.

**Graduation changes the market model.** The game seeds an engine market asset from FROG/BONK parameters and transfers book cash and tokens into an account-backed liquidity book ([graduate.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/graduate.ts#L37), [graduate.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/graduate.ts#L63)). A new isolated probe graduated matched 0% and 5% allocation coins. Both started at the same $0.0002159562 engine quote and $43,296.72 book cash; the book held 200M tokens at 0% versus 150M at 5%. One engine step with no orders moved both quotes −0.154%, while book cash and token quantity stayed unchanged. Actual $1,000 market buys moved both marks +0.670%; $1,000 sells moved both −0.660%, with book cash and quantity changing in the corresponding direction. In these isolated market-order probes, 25% less book inventory did not change the observed quote impact because the asset’s `advNotional` is based on raised cash, not book token quantity ([graduate.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/graduate.ts#L73), [graduate.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/graduate.ts#L82)). The engine can change the mark between trades, so graduated coins do not follow a reserve-only quote. The new probe and snapshot aggregation are reproducible with [launchpad-npc-audit-probe.ts](../../scripts/research/launchpad-npc-audit-probe.ts) and its saved [results](../../proof/economy-audit/launchpad-npc-audit.json). Pump’s official public program docs describe completing the bonding curve then migrating liquidity to PumpSwap, and its official Swap docs describe PumpSwap as a constant-product AMM ([PUMP program](https://github.com/pump-fun/pump-public-docs/blob/main/docs/PUMP_PROGRAM_README.md), [PumpSwap](https://github.com/pump-fun/pump-public-docs/blob/main/docs/PUMP_SWAP_README.md)). The current build is an approximation, not that post-graduation mechanism.

Creator allocation is minted at launch and limited to 50M (5% supply); total creator allocation plus curve buys is capped at 100M, with a vesting schedule ([launch.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/launch.ts#L198), [constants.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/constants.ts#L19), [constants.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/constants.ts#L46)). Graduation seeds the book with `SUPPLY - CURVE_SUPPLY - allocation`, so a maximum allocation lowers book float from 200M to 150M, while the $ raised and initial quote remain determined by the same curve ([graduate.ts](../../src/world/launchpad/graduate.ts#L82)). That gives a creator free ownership while reducing the book’s token depth. The vesting/rug controls are meaningful mitigations, but the allocation choice is economically consequential and should be priced/represented explicitly in graduation liquidity and ownership views.

## What should count as acceptance

1. Keep persistent account ownership through every curve buy/sell, migration, cancellation, and graduation; reconcile per-account cash plus inventory and explicit faucets/sinks. Report legacy outside-money flows separately from funded trader P&L.
2. Make paid promotion spend for attention with uncertain reach and delayed, finite-budget conversion. A promotion must not guarantee an instant buy funded by a faucet. Compare the payer’s cost, NPC cash changes, price path, and player impact over repeated seeds.
3. Make ordinary positive and negative news produce directionally distinct responses. A player reply or message should affect demand only when its modeled audience/attention path says so; otherwise label it as social-only in the UI.
4. Demonstrate price reactivity with held-out controlled triggers: player buy/sell, creator sale, positive/negative news, credible social signal, liquidity drain, and market-wide shock. Use event ablations and per-account decision traces, not only volatility quantiles or historical price-path similarity.
5. Evolve beyond historical replay as the main motion source: per-agent attention, conviction/trust, cost-basis/loss memory, holdings/cash constraints, and portfolio-aware actions. Historical episodes can remain a timing/flow prior, but should not substitute for the agent response model.
6. Either implement a post-graduation pool whose quote and fills follow its reserves, or describe and validate the current bounded book as an intentional approximation. Test no-trade marks, creator allocation extremes (0%/5%), fill depth, and curve-to-book quote continuity separately.

## Evidence limits

This is a source and snapshot audit, not an external historical-fit claim. The 96-episode library is curated and oversamples active launches; the project’s historical-flow document explicitly says timestamps are collector timestamps and the work is not a full statistical fit or exact replay. No broad live-world economic conclusion follows from one snapshot. The current official Pump public docs support the cited curve/migration/AMM comparison; they do not establish that this game must reproduce every present-day product rule.
