# 01 — Markets & Economy

Owner: Quant (market simulation engine). Status: draft v1, 2026-10-07.
Scope: every asset class and how its price is generated, the business catalog, the macro layer, how players move markets, and how the multiplayer money supply stays balanced.

All companies, countries, currencies and tokens are fictional. No real-market data, no real money, no advice framing.

---

## 0. Design principles

1. **Explainable, not random.** Every price move must be traceable to a cause a player could have seen or inferred: a macro print, an earnings surprise, a news event, a sector rotation, or another player's order flow. Pure noise exists but is small. This is what makes information, data and AI valuable (the whole AI layer depends on it).
2. **One world, one engine.** All asset classes share one macro state (rates, inflation, growth, risk appetite). A rate hike hits bonds, real estate, growth stocks, crypto and the player's mortgage the same tick. Cross-asset consequences are the depth.
3. **Players are price-makers, not just price-takers.** Small players are invisible; whales move prices; coordinated players can squeeze. Impact follows a real law (square-root impact) so it scales sensibly from $1k to $10B.
4. **Fairness by batching.** Orders clear in per-tick batch auctions, so latency, device and connection quality never matter. Mobile players are not disadvantaged.
5. **Deterministic and seeded.** The world sim is a pure function of (seed, previous state, player orders in this tick). Replays, balance runs, anti-cheat and server recovery all rely on this.
6. **Money is scarce on purpose.** Faucets and sinks are measured every tick and steered by an automatic stabilizer so a dollar in year 3 of the world is worth the same as a dollar on launch day.

## 1. Time model

| Unit | Real time | Game time |
|---|---|---|
| Tick | 5 s | ~5 game-minutes during market hours |
| Market day | 1 h | 1 trading day (open, session, close auction) |
| Week | 5 h | 5 trading days + 2 h "weekend" (stocks closed, crypto/real estate keep running) |
| Quarter | ~3 days | Earnings season, central bank meetings |
| Game year | ~15 days | Business cycle phase can change |
| Season (ladder period) | 8 weeks (~3.7 game years) | The world never resets; a season only frames the seasonal ladders, AUC halvings and season-level stats. Long enough for one full boom-bust cycle |

- Every asset has a session: stocks and bonds trade 08:00–16:00 game time with open and close auctions; forex 24/5; crypto 24/7; real estate and private companies trade continuously but slowly (listings take hours to sell).
- **Offline:** businesses keep earning for up to 8 real hours (upgradable to 16 h with a manager), then stop. Open positions keep marking to market; stop-loss and take-profit orders still fire. This caps the idle faucet (see §8).
- Annualized numbers in this doc are per **game year**.

## 2. Price engine core

Every tradeable asset `i` has three layers of price:

1. **Fundamental value `V_i`**: what it is worth given its cash flows and the macro state (earnings × multiple, rent / cap rate, cost of production, interest-rate parity...). Moves slowly and explainably.
2. **Market price `P_i`**: what it trades at. Pulled toward `V_i` with a mean-reversion half-life, pushed by factor returns, news, jumps and order flow.
3. **Quote**: NPC market makers quote a bid/ask around `P_i` with depth. Players trade against that book and against each other.

### 2.1 Per-tick log return

```
r_i(t) = κ_i · ln(V_i / P_i) · dt                      # pull toward fundamental
       + Σ_k β_ik · f_k(t)                             # factor returns (market, sector, rates, risk appetite, region, FX)
       + σ_i(t) · √dt · ε_i(t)                         # idiosyncratic noise, ε ~ Student-t(ν=4) for fat tails
       + J_i(t)                                        # jumps: news, earnings, crashes
       + I_i(t)                                        # net impact of order flow this tick (§7)
```

- `κ_i` = ln 2 / half-life. Large caps: 20 game days. Small caps: 60. Crypto: 120 (it can stay irrational a long time). Bonds: 2 (anchored hard to the yield curve).
- **Volatility clustering** with GARCH(1,1): `σ²(t) = ω + α·ε²(t−1) + β·σ²(t−1)`, defaults α = 0.08, β = 0.90. Calm stays calm, panic stays panicky.
- **Jumps**: compound Poisson. Per-asset base jump intensity λ (e.g. 3 per game year for a stock), size ~ double-exponential (Kou model) with downside jumps larger than upside (mean down-jump 6%, mean up-jump 4%). News events override the base jumps with targeted jumps (§6).
- **Factor model** (`f_k`): global market, 12 sector factors, 5 regional factors, rates (Δ10y yield), risk appetite (the "Fear Index", game VIX), USD-equivalent strength, crypto beta. Factors are themselves generated by the macro layer (§5), so one recession moves everything coherently.
- **Correlation regimes**: in a crisis, factor loadings to the global market are multiplied by 1.5 and idiosyncratic noise is cut. Diversification fails exactly when players need it most, like in reality.
- **Seeded RNG**: xoshiro128** (the 32-bit member of the xoshiro family; 256** needs BigInt and is ~10× slower in JS, see docs/ENGINE.md) with one stream per asset, per sector and per macro region, derived from the world seed, so adding a new asset doesn't change other assets' paths.

### 2.2 Circuit breakers and sanity bounds

- Single stock: trading halts for 10 real minutes after a ±15% move inside one day; reopens with an auction.
- Index: market-wide halt at −7%, −13%, −20% in a day (mirrors real rules).
- Hard floor: no price below 0.01; bankruptcy is an event (§3.1), not a drift to zero.
- Fundamental guards: P/E can never sustainably exceed 3× the sector band (the pull term grows).

## 3. Asset classes

Unlock order follows the player's **license tier** (fits Velvet's progression): Cash account → Retail broker (stocks, ETFs, crypto) → Pro (forex, commodities, bonds, margin 2×) → Derivatives (options, futures, perps, up to 10×) → Institutional (private deals, IPO underwriting, structured products, OTC blocks). Licenses cost money and have a background check (ties to Tape's legal record: convicted players lose licenses).

### 3.1 Stocks

**Universe at launch:** ~120 NPC companies across 12 sectors and 5 regions, plus player companies that IPO (§4.6). Each NPC company is a little simulated business:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Revenue `R`, growth `g` | Grows with sector demand × regional GDP, plus company-specific drift |
| Margin `m` | Sector base ± quality; squeezed by input costs (commodities) and wages (inflation) |
| Shares outstanding | Changes with buybacks, issuance, splits |
| Debt `D` | Interest cost follows rates; high debt = bankruptcy risk in recessions |
| Quality, Management, Moat | Hidden 0–100 stats; revealed partially by research/data (AI hook) |
| Sector P/E band | e.g. Utilities 12–18, Tech 25–60, Banks 8–12 |

**Fundamental value:**
```
EPS = (R · m − D · (rate + credit_spread)) · (1 − tax) / shares
V   = EPS · PE_sector(macro) · (1 + growth_premium) · sentiment_multiplier
PE_sector(macro) = PE_base · exp(−d · (r_10y − r_neutral)) · (1 + 0.3 · risk_appetite)
```
Duration `d` is high for growth sectors (tech, biotech) and low for value (utilities, staples), so rate hikes crush growth stocks first. That's a lesson the game teaches without saying it.

**Earnings:** every quarter (~3 real days). Actual EPS = expected EPS × (1 + surprise), surprise ~ N(bias_quality, 6%). Price jump on release = surprise × earnings sensitivity (≈ 2–4× surprise) plus guidance. Analysts (NPC) publish consensus estimates; better data/AI gives a sharper estimate of the true number before release.

**Corporate events:** dividends (paid in cash, a faucet tied to real earnings), buybacks, stock splits at price > 1,000, secondary offerings (dilution), M&A (NPC acquires NPC at 20–40% premium; players can acquire NPC companies by buying >50%, see §7.5), CEO scandals, bankruptcy (equity goes to ~0, bondholders recover 20–60%), spin-offs.

**Indices & ETFs:** a global index (GX 100), regional indices, 12 sector ETFs, a dividend ETF, a crypto basket. ETFs are priced at NAV ± tiny premium; NPC arbitrageurs keep them in line, so there is no free ETF-vs-basket arbitrage after fees.

**Short selling:** allowed from Pro tier. Borrow fee 0.5–30%/yr depending on short interest; hard-to-borrow names can be recalled; short squeezes happen naturally when short interest > 30% of float and price rises (forced buy-ins feed the impact model).

**Example NPC companies** (one per sector to set the tone):
- Halcyon Systems (Tech, cloud software, high P/E, rate-sensitive)
- Verdane Energy (Energy, oil & gas, tracks crude)
- Ostrava Steel (Industrials, cyclical, commodity input costs)
- Lumen Pharma (Health, binary drug-trial events, jumpy)
- Meridia Bank (Financials, profits rise with rate spreads, crash in credit crises)
- Corvo Motors (Consumer discretionary, luxury EVs, meme-prone)
- Pallas Foods (Staples, boring, defensive)
- Aurelius Holdings (Real estate developer, rate-sensitive, leveraged)
- Nimbus Compute (AI/semis, the "GPU shortage" stock, ties to compute prices)
- Sentinel Cyber (Cybersecurity, revenue rises when hacks rise in the world, see §6)
- Atlas Freight (Logistics, tracks trade volume and fuel)
- Velour Maison (Luxury goods, tracks top-1% wealth in the game)

That last one is a fun loop: luxury stocks literally go up when players spend on lifestyle.

### 3.3 Crypto

Separate, wilder market. 24/7. ~15 NPC tokens at launch:

- **Bitcoin-like store of value** ("Aurum Coin", AUC): fixed supply, halving every season (supply-shock narrative each season, a recurring event players learn to anticipate).
- **Smart-contract platform** ("Lattice", LTX): price tied to on-chain activity index, which players' DeFi use raises.
- **Stablecoins** ("GUSD"): pegged at 1.00; tiny depeg risk events (P ≈ 0.3/season) that crash to 0.85–0.97 then recover or die.
- **Meme coins**: spawn and die constantly (Poisson, ~2 per game week), ruled by the Hype model below; 90% go to ~0 within a season.
- **AI tokens** ("Synapse", SYN): price linked to the global AI race index, i.e. to how much players spend on compute.

**Crypto price model** = core model with: higher base vol (60–150%/yr vs 20–40% for stocks), crypto factor beta 0.7–1.3, weaker pull to a fuzzy fundamental (network activity × adoption), plus a **Hype state** `H ∈ [0,1]`:
```
dH = a · (social_mentions + player_inflow − H) dt − b · H dt + shocks
drift_bonus = c · H     vol_multiplier = 1 + 2H
```
Hype is fed by news, by player net buying (reflexive), and by player "shill" campaigns (Media businesses can spend to raise hype; doing it while holding the coin is market manipulation, see Tape).

**Player-launched tokens** (Institutional tier, late game): a player can launch a token for a big fee. Rug-pulling it is possible and very illegal: high detection, big court case. Huge drama potential, needs Tape's review.

### 3.4 Commodities

Spot plus a futures curve (1, 3, 6, 12-month). Price driven by a supply/demand inventory model rather than pure noise:

```
inventory(t+1) = inventory(t) + supply(t) − demand(t)
demand = base · GDP_factor · seasonality(month) · (P/P_ref)^(−elasticity)
supply = capacity · utilization(P) + shocks (storms, strikes, wars, embargoes)
ln P mean-reverts (Ornstein-Uhlenbeck) toward marginal cost of production, shifted by days-of-cover:
   target = cost · (days_cover / normal_cover)^(−γ)
```
- Low inventories → backwardation (futures below spot), spikes. High inventories → contango; storage costs make "buy and sit on futures" lose money.
- Launch set: Crude oil, Natural gas, Gold, Silver, Copper, Lithium, Rare earths, Wheat, Corn, Coffee, Cocoa, Lumber, Uranium.
- **Game-native commodities** (the interesting ones):
  - **Compute**: canonical engine unit is the **PFLOP-day**; the market quotes **$/GPU-hour** (`1 PFLOP-day = 10 GPUh`, per RESOLVED-NUMBERS). Reference prices: spot $0.12/GPUh ($1.2/PFLOP-day), reserved 30-day contract $0.20/GPUh, owned-GPU energy ≈ $0.015/GPUh (floats with the Energy MWh price). Spot price model in §3.4.1. Everyone training AI bids for it; Ledger's AI tree consumes it.
  - **Data (data credits)**: generated by Media, Fintech, Retail-app businesses; sold on a data exchange; quality grades. Stolen data floods the market and depresses its price (ties to Tape).
  - **Energy (MWh)**: Data centers consume it; Energy businesses produce it. A heat wave + AI boom = power-price spike.
- Gold is the crisis hedge: positive beta to Fear Index, negative to real rates.

#### 3.4.1 Compute (GPU index) price model

```
D_world  = D_npc(cycle, AI-race index) + D_players            # PFLOP-days per game day
D_players = Σ active training runs · PFLOP-days per run / run_days
S_world  = S_npc_cloud + Σ player data-center capacity (+ player-to-player leasing)
u        = D_world / S_world                                  # utilization
spot $/GPUh = 0.12 · (u / 0.75)^3   clamped to [$0.05, $0.60]   # convex: tight markets spike hard
S_npc_cloud grows toward demand with a ~2 game-week lag when u > 0.85, shrinks when u < 0.6
```
- **Sizing:** NPC demand is set so players are ~10–20% of world compute demand in a normal season. Player demand then moves price mostly in aggregate (an AI boom across the server) and in shortages, not because one rival trains a model.
- **Per-run demand** follows Ledger's C3 table (05 v2): T0 100–1k PFLOP-days per run (1k–10k GPUh), T1 1k–5k, T2 15k–60k, T3 200k–800k, T4 4M–12M, T5 35M–120M (350M–1.2B GPUh). At the flat $0.12/GPUh spot price, a T0 run costs $120–$1.2k and a T5 run costs $42M–$144M.
- **World demand estimate** (an assumption until Forge's telemetry exists): tier mix T0 40% / T1 30% / T2 18% / T3 8% / T4 3% / T5 1%, with T0–T2 running one run per real day, T3 every 3 days, T4 weekly and T5 every 2 weeks. At mid-range run sizes that is ≈ 110M PFLOP-days per real day per 1,000 AI agents, so ≈ 2.2B PFLOP-days (22B GPUh, about $2.7B of spending) per real day for 20k agents. T4 and T5 runs make up about 80% of it, so the compute market is driven by the top of the server.
- **NPC demand** is therefore set at about 9–20B PFLOP-days per real day, so players stay at 10–20% of the world total. Both scale with activity-weighted players (Forge 02 §1). Because frontier runs are big and lumpy, a cluster of T5 runs starting together can now push utilization and spot price visibly, which is intended. The spot formula's convexity is capped by the $0.60 ceiling and reserved contracts.
- **Data Center capacity**: a base T4 Data Center ($40M, 48 h payback at ≈ $0.10/GPUh margin) produces ≈ 0.84M PFLOP-days per real hour (≈ 20M per real day). Player demand alone (20k agents) equals about 110 base data centers, so owning compute is a real business at the top end.
- **Chip-shortage event** (Ledger 05 §1): spot ×1.5–×4 for 7–30 days through the same formula (supply shock), new GPU orders get 14–90 day lead times, reserved contracts keep their price. Players who own GPUs can lease them to rivals at market price.
- **World DU cap breach** (Forge, 3.0M DU/tick): when the world's AI budget is scaled down, the engine raises `D_npc` by 10% for that game day, so it reads as a diegetic "compute crunch" and the spot price rises.
- **Data Center (T4 business)** sells at spot minus 5% to the world pool. Its capacity is sized so its base payback holds at a $0.12 reference price.

### 3.5 Forex

Five fictional currency blocs: **Nordic Crown (NKR)**, **Federal Dollar (FDL, reserve currency, the game's unit of account)**, **Pacific Yuan (PYN)**, **Euran (EUN)**, **Southern Real (SRL, emerging, high yield, high vol)**.

```
d ln S = (i_domestic − i_foreign) · dt  [carry, via forward]   ← covered interest parity holds by construction
       + θ · (ln PPP_fair − ln S) dt                              ← slow purchasing-power pull (half-life ~1.5 game yrs)
       + β_risk · Δrisk_appetite                                  ← EM currencies fall in risk-off, reserve rises
       + news jumps (rate surprises, elections, capital controls)
```
- **Carry trade** works on average and blows up in crises (SRL can drop 25% in a week): a real lesson with real risk.
- Cross rates are computed from the five vs FDL, so triangular arbitrage is impossible by construction.
- Businesses and properties located in a region earn in its currency; players can hedge.

### 3.6 Bonds & rates

**Yield curve per region** with Nelson-Siegel:
```
y(τ) = L + S · (1 − e^(−τ/λ))/(τ/λ) + C · ((1 − e^(−τ/λ))/(τ/λ) − e^(−τ/λ))
L (level)  = r_neutral + expected inflation + term premium
S (slope)  = policy_rate − L   (inverts before recessions, a learnable signal)
C (curve)  = noise + crisis hump
```
- Instruments: 3-month bills (cash-like), 2y, 10y, 30y government bonds; corporate bonds per NPC company (yield = govt + credit spread, spread = f(leverage, sector, cycle)); high-yield ETF; inflation-linked bonds.
- Bond prices are computed exactly from yields (duration/convexity), so a player who understands duration can hedge real-estate rate risk with bonds.
- **Defaults**: corporate default probability per tick from a Merton-style distance-to-default; recovery 20–60%.
- **Player-issued bonds** (late game): a player company can borrow from the market; its credit spread depends on its leverage, profits and legal record. Defaulting is a court event.

### 3.7 Derivatives (Derivatives license)

- **Options** on indices, large stocks, gold, oil, AUC. Priced by Black-Scholes with an NPC **implied vol surface**: `IV(K,T) = σ_realized_expected · (1 + skew · ln(K/F) + term structure)`; skew steepens in fear. NPC market makers delta-hedge, which feeds back into the underlying (gamma effects: big player call buying can cause a gamma squeeze; this is emergent, not scripted).
- **Futures** on indices, commodities, FX, compute. Daily mark-to-market and margin.
- **Perpetual swaps** on crypto with funding rate = clamp(premium of perp over spot, ±0.1%/8h). Up to 10× (20× institutional).
- **Margin & liquidation**: initial margin 1/leverage, maintenance 50% of initial; below it the engine liquidates at market with a 1% penalty to an insurance fund (a sink). No negative balances carried past a liquidation; if gap moves cause a deficit, it becomes debt to the "Game Clearing House" and blocks withdrawals/spending until repaid.
- **Structured products** (Institutional): a player can sell covered calls, principal-protected notes, etc. to NPC buyers.
- Not included: binary options, prediction/event betting markets (too close to gambling; open question for Mathis).

### 3.8 Real estate

A physical, local, illiquid market, the backbone of steady wealth.

- **Map**: 5 regions → 3 cities each (15 cities) → 6–10 districts each. Example: Port Avalon (FDL bloc) with Old Harbor, Financial Mile, Glasshill, Riverside Lofts, Industrial Basin, Summit Estates.
- **Property types**: Studio, Apartment, Townhouse, Apartment block, Office floor, Office tower, Retail unit, Shopping mall, Warehouse, Data-center site, Hotel, Villa, Mansion, Private island (unique, auctioned).
- **Each property** has: size, condition (0–100, decays), district desirability, occupancy, rent, upkeep, property tax.

```
Rent       = district_rent_index · size · condition_mult · amenity_mult
NOI        = Rent · occupancy − upkeep − property_tax − management
Cap rate   = policy_rate_long + district_risk_premium + illiquidity_premium (≈ 1.5–6%)
Value      = NOI / Cap rate · (1 + expected rent growth / cap rate adjustment)
district_rent_index grows with: regional GDP, population/inflows, player investment in the district (gentrification), crime/scandal events (negative)
```
- **Player effect on districts**: when players renovate, build offices, open luxury shops, the district desirability score rises → rent and values rise for all owners there. Early movers into an up-and-coming district win. This is an organic, multiplayer-visible "neighborhood" game.
- **Liquidity**: selling lists the property; time-to-sell ~ exponential with mean 2–24 real hours depending on price vs appraisal (sell at −10% to sell instantly to an NPC fund). Buyers pay 3% transaction cost + 1% stamp duty (sinks).
- **Mortgages**: LTV up to 80% (residential) / 65% (commercial); rate = 10y yield + 1.5–3%; floating or fixed. Rising rates + falling values can push LTV over limits → margin call → forced sale. Leverage is how players get rich fast and how they go broke.
- **Development**: buy land, pay construction over time (2–10 real days), sell or hold. Construction cost tracks Lumber/Copper/Steel prices and wages.
- **REITs**: listed real-estate funds as an easy liquid alternative.
- **Housing crisis event**: rates spike + bank failures → transaction volume freezes, values −20–35%, then a multi-season recovery.

### 3.9 Private markets & startups (Institutional)

- NPC startups appear each week with a pitch (sector, team score, burn rate, round size). Outcomes follow a **power law**: 65% fail, 25% return 0.5–3×, 9% return 3–20×, 1% return 20–200× over 1–3 seasons (Pareto α ≈ 1.1).
- Players can invest in seed/Series A/B rounds; better research/data AI improves the odds of picking winners by raising the visible team/market scores' accuracy.
- Players can also **found a startup** by spending on R&D; a winning startup can be sold to an NPC acquirer or IPO'd.

### 3.10 Collectibles as assets (touches Velvet)

Watches, art, classic cars, wine, rare sneakers. Each item has rarity, condition, provenance:
- **Mass luxury** (new Rolex-like watches, new supercars, standard yachts): depreciate 10–40% on purchase, then slowly. Pure consumption = sink.
- **Rare/limited** (numbered pieces, art, classic cars): price index driven by top-1% wealth growth and a fashion/hype cycle; can appreciate. Bought/sold at auctions (seller fee 12%, buyer premium 15%: big sink).
- Insurance and storage fees, theft events (ties to Tape's insurance).

## 4. Businesses

Businesses are the steady engine of wealth (the "tycoon" half). Markets are the volatile half. A good player uses business cash flow to fund investments, and investments to buy bigger businesses.

### 4.1 How a business earns

Every business sits in a **city** and a **sector market**. Each city/sector has a demand pool shared by every business in it, player and NPC alike:

```
Demand_city,sector(t) = base_demand · population · GDP_factor(region) · cycle_sensitivity · seasonality · event_shocks
Attractiveness u_j    = a·ln(quality_j) + b·ln(1 + marketing_j) − c·ln(price_j / ref_price) + d·location_j + brand_j
Market share s_j      = exp(u_j) / Σ_k exp(u_k)          (logit over all competitors in that city/sector)
Units sold            = min(Demand · s_j, capacity_j)
Revenue               = Units · price_j
Profit                = Revenue − COGS(commodity inputs) − wages(inflation-linked) − rent − energy − upkeep − tax
```

- This makes businesses **genuinely multiplayer**: if three players open cafés on Glasshill, they split the demand. Moving to an under-served city or out-competing on quality/marketing/price is real strategy.
- NPC competitors always exist (they take 30–60% share in a fresh city) so a solo player isn't "monopoly by default," and they slowly exit when players dominate.
- **Price setting**: players can set price ±30% of reference. Higher price = more margin, less share. Optimal price is a simple calculus problem; a trading/business AI can find it (AI hook).
- **Input costs** are tied to the commodity market (coffee → cafés, steel → auto factories, energy → data centers), so commodity hedging matters for big owners.

### 4.2 Upgrade axes

Every business has the same six axes so the UI stays learnable:

| Axis | Effect per level | Cost of level n |
|---|---|---|
| Capacity | +12% max units | `C0 · 0.10 · 1.18^n` |
| Quality | +1 quality step (feeds share) | `C0 · 0.12 · 1.20^n` |
| Efficiency | −4% COGS and energy | `C0 · 0.10 · 1.22^n` |
| Marketing | spend per hour, diminishing (`ln(1+m)`) | flow cost, not one-off |
| Automation | −6% wages, longer offline cap | `C0 · 0.15 · 1.25^n`, AI-manager levels need Ledger's AI tree |
| Expansion | open the same business in another city (a chain) | 80% of C0 per new city, + brand bonus shared across the chain |

- Levels cap at 25 per axis; exponential costs plus logit share give strong **diminishing returns**, which pushes players to diversify into new sectors and cities instead of maxing one thing forever (core anti-inflation lever, §8).
- **Managers**: hire NPC managers (salary, skill 1–5 stars) to raise offline cap and auto-run marketing/pricing. A trained AI agent can replace them later.
- **Condition**: every business decays 1%/day without upkeep; low condition lowers quality and triggers inspection fines.

### 4.3 Synergies

- **Vertical integration**: owning an input (coffee plantation → cafés, lithium mine → EV factory, solar farm → data center) gives that input at cost, no market price risk.
- **Clusters**: 3+ businesses in one district give +5% demand to each (foot traffic).
- **Conglomerate**: 5+ sectors owned → small brand bonus and access to a "holding company" structure needed for IPO.
- **Data generation**: consumer apps, delivery, social, fintech, retail chains generate data credits as a by-product (fuel for AI training or sale on the data exchange).

### 4.5 Business events

Per business per game week, small chance of: viral moment (+30% demand for 2 days), bad review, health inspection (fine if condition < 50), staff strike (if wages lag inflation), fire/flood (insurance claim), new NPC competitor opening, supply shortage, celebrity endorsement, data breach (if hackable). Events are where luck lives so markets can stay explainable.

### 4.6 Selling, valuing and IPO-ing a business

- **Valuation**: `EV = trailing 7-day profit × sector multiple × growth adj`, shown live.
- **Sell to NPC buyer**: instant at 85% of EV (the 15% haircut is a sink and stops buy-upgrade-sell loops from printing money).
- **Sell to a player**: any price, via a contract (Forge's P2P system), with a 2% transaction tax and anti-funneling rules (§9).
- **IPO (holding company)**: a player with ≥ $50M of businesses in a holding can list it. Underwriting fee 5% (sink). The company becomes a real stock other players can buy; its earnings are the player's actual business profits; the founder keeps ≥ 51% or risks a takeover. Insider trading on your own company is the classic legal trap (Tape). This turns top players into stocks the whole server can bet on or short — a big social hook.

## 5. Macro layer

### 5.1 Regions

Five economic blocs, each with its own currency, central bank, cities and personality:

| Bloc | Currency | Personality |
|---|---|---|
| Federal Union | FDL (reserve) | Big, tech-heavy, deep markets |
| Euran Compact | EUN | Slow growth, strong luxury and industry |
| Pacific League | PYN | Manufacturing and chips, export-driven |
| Nordic Alliance | NKR | Energy and wealth, low volatility |
| Southern Coalition | SRL | Emerging, commodities, high growth, high risk, high rates |

### 5.2 State variables per region (updated every market day)

GDP growth `g`, output gap `x`, inflation `π`, inflation expectations `π_e`, unemployment `u`, policy rate `i`, credit conditions `c`, consumer sentiment, housing index. Global: Risk appetite / Fear Index (0–100), AI-race index, world trade volume, world hack rate, geopolitical tension.

### 5.3 Business cycle

A 4-state Markov regime per region, correlated across regions through a global regime:

| Regime | GDP g | Inflation | Equities drift | Typical length |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Expansion | +3% | ~2% | +10%/yr | 1.5–3 game yrs |
| Overheating | +4% | 3–6% rising | +4%, vol rising | 0.5–1 yr |
| Contraction | −2% | falling | −20 to −35% | 0.5–1 yr |
| Recovery | +2% from low base | low | +25%/yr | 0.5–1 yr |

Transition probabilities rise with imbalances (credit growth, asset bubbles measured by aggregate P/E vs history, inflation gap), so recessions are **caused**, not random — a careful player can see one building (inverted yield curve, P/E stretched, credit spreads widening).

### 5.4 Central banks (Taylor rule + personality)

```
i* = r_neutral + π + 0.5 · (π − π_target) + 0.5 · x
i(t+1) = i(t) + clamp(ρ · (i* − i(t)), ±0.50%)      # moves in 0.25% steps at scheduled meetings
```
- Meetings every ~6 real days; a forward-guidance statement is released 1 day before (a hawk/dove score players can read).
- Emergency cuts and QE (bond buying lowers long yields) in crashes.
- Each bank has a hidden "hawkishness" trait; reading it is an analysis skill.

### 5.5 Inflation

- Two inflations: **world inflation** (the macro π that drives rates and NPC prices) and **game monetary inflation** (how much money players have created). The stabilizer (§8) keeps the second near 0 in real terms; the first is flavor and strategy.
- World inflation raises wages, rents, input costs and lifestyle-item prices; it erodes cash. Holding pure cash for a season is a mild loss, which pushes players to invest.

### 5.6 Crises and crashes

Rare, telegraphed-but-not-scheduled, multi-asset events. Probability per season roughly 35% for at least one major crisis. Each has an anatomy (trigger → contagion → policy response → recovery):

| Crisis | Trigger | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Equity bubble burst | Aggregate P/E > 1.6× norm + rate hikes | Index −30–45% over 2–6 weeks, tech −60%, gold +15% |
| Credit crunch | Defaults spike in a contraction | Spreads ×3, banks −50%, real estate −25%, mortgages tighten (LTV −20 pts) |
| Crypto winter | Stablecoin depeg or exchange collapse | Crypto −70–90%, hype → 0, lasts a season |
| Oil shock | War/embargo event | Crude +80%, airlines −40%, inflation +3 pts, rates up |
| Currency crisis | SRL debt + risk-off | SRL −40%, capital controls (can't withdraw SRL assets for 3 days) |
| AI bubble | AI-race index overextended | Compute price −60%, Nimbus/semis −70%, AI tokens −85% |
| Cyber pandemic | Massive world hack wave (players causing it counts) | Security stocks +50%, everything else −10%, insurance claims surge |
| Flash crash | Liquidity gap + liquidation cascade | −10% in minutes, recovers 70% within a day; circuit breakers fire |

Crash days are when fortunes are made (cash-rich players buy) and lost (leveraged players get liquidated). Leaderboards churn, which keeps the top from freezing.

## 6. News & information

### 6.1 News events

Every event has a structured payload so the engine, UI and AI all read the same thing:
```
{ id, type, headline, body, targets: [asset|sector|region], impact_vector: {asset: expected_move},
  magnitude_dist, credibility (0–1), lead_time, decay_half_life, is_rumor, source }
```
- Types: macro data (CPI, jobs, GDP), central bank, earnings & guidance, product launch, drug trial, lawsuit, M&A, CEO scandal, regulation, weather, war/geopolitics, supply disruption, crypto hack, celebrity tweet, player-driven (a player's IPO, a big hack, a court verdict, a whale buy).
- Generator: a template library (~400 templates) × fictional entities × the current macro state, so headlines are coherent ("Meridia Bank shares slide as Federal Union curve inverts").
- **Price reaction** = expected move × credibility, part immediately (jump), part drifting over `decay_half_life` (post-announcement drift, which skilled players and AIs exploit).

### 6.2 Information has tiers (this is what makes data and AI worth money)

| Tier | Source | What you get | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free | Public feed | Headlines when they hit | Free |
| Terminal | Paid subscription | Full body, consensus estimates, macro calendar, 5-second earlier delivery | $/day scales with net worth |
| Research | Analysts, data purchases, own AI | Noisy estimate of hidden variables (next earnings, company quality, central bank hawkishness) | Data credits + compute |
| Rumors | Social feed | Leading signals, 40–70% accurate | Free, unreliable |
| Insider | Hacking a company or player, bribery | Exact upcoming numbers | Illegal: court risk (Tape) |

Signal math: research gives `estimate = truth + N(0, σ_research)`, with σ shrinking as the player's data and model quality rise. That's the cleanest way to make "train a better AI" pay off in markets without guaranteed profits: it narrows uncertainty, it never removes it.

### 6.3 Players can make news

Big player actions publish automatically: a $100M+ trade in one name ("Whale accumulates Corvo Motors"), IPOs, takeovers, court verdicts, major hacks, bankruptcies. News outlets owned by players can publish opinion pieces that nudge sentiment slightly. Lying in them is manipulation.

## 7. How players move markets

### 7.1 Market structure: per-tick batch auction

- Each tick (5 s) the engine collects all orders, then clears each asset in one uniform-price call auction against (a) other players' orders and (b) NPC liquidity.
- No latency race, no front-running inside a tick, same result on desktop and phone. Also cheap to compute and deterministic.
- Order types: market, limit, stop, stop-limit, take-profit, trailing stop, good-till-cancel; iceberg and TWAP/VWAP execution algos at Pro tier (these are what trading AIs use to hide size).

### 7.2 NPC liquidity

NPC market makers post a ladder around `P_i`:
```
half-spread  = base_spread_i · (1 + vol_multiplier · σ_i) · (1 + fear)
depth at level k = ADV_i · depth_frac · e^(−k·δ)
```
Typical spreads: mega-cap stock 2 bps, small cap 25 bps, major FX 1 bp, AUC 5 bps, meme coin 1–3%, real estate 3–6% (through transaction costs).
NPC depth thins in crises (`depth × (1 − 0.6·fear)`), so liquidity vanishes exactly when everyone wants out.

### 7.3 Price impact (square-root law)

For a net order imbalance `Q` in a tick:
```
temporary impact  ΔP/P = Y · σ_daily · sign(Q) · √(|Q| / ADV)        Y ≈ 0.8
permanent share   ≈ 50% of temporary; the rest decays with half-life ~ 30 min game time
```
Example: buying 1% of a stock's daily volume at once moves it ≈ 0.8 × 2% × 0.1 = 0.16%. Buying 25% of ADV at once moves it ≈ 0.8%. Buying 200% of ADV of a small cap with 4% daily vol ≈ 0.8 × 4% × 1.41 ≈ +4.5%, on top of crossing a thin book. Small players: invisible. Whales: must work orders over time, which creates visible footprints others (and their AIs) can trade against.

### 7.4 Feedback from players to fundamentals

- Aggregate player flow shifts the **sentiment multiplier** on fundamental value (bounded ±25%), so a sustained crowd can drive a bubble, and the pull to fundamentals eventually deflates it.
- Player businesses change sector demand, district rents, compute supply, energy demand, data supply.
- Player lifestyle spending raises the Luxury sector and collectible index.
- Player hacking raises the world hack rate (Security stocks up, insurance premiums up for everyone).
- Player AI training raises compute prices and the AI-race index.

### 7.5 Corporate control

- Owning ≥ 5% of a company: public disclosure (a news event).
- ≥ 20%: board seat (vote on dividends/buybacks).
- ≥ 50%: control. An NPC company can be absorbed into your holding (its profits become business income; you can merge it with your businesses). A player company can be taken over: hostile takeovers are a non-hacking way to bring down a rich player, with defenses (poison pill costs, buying back float, white knight allies).

### 7.6 Position and leverage limits (anti-domination)

- Max position per asset: 25% of float for NPC companies, 49% for player companies held by another player (unless takeover bid).
- Max order per tick: 3× ADV; larger orders are auto-split.
- Short interest cap 50% of float per name.
- Leverage caps by license tier, and lower for players with active court cases.

## 8. Money supply & anti-inflation

This is the most important balance problem in any multiplayer economy game. Most of them die from runaway inflation: the top player's income dwarfs the economy, prices become meaningless, new players can't matter.

### 8.1 Faucets (money created) and sinks (money destroyed)

| Faucets | Sinks |
|---|---|
| Starting cash ($5,000), one-time Retail-broker onboarding grant (≈ $250/player, Velvet 04 §4.2), quest rewards | Taxes: income (business profit) and capital gains, progressive |
| Business profits (NPC customers pay players) | Trading fees and spreads paid to NPC makers |
| Dividends, coupons, rent from NPC tenants | Transaction costs and stamp duty on property |
| NPC side of player trades (equity drift is a net transfer from the NPC world) | Upkeep, condition decay, staff wages, energy |
| Hedge fund fees on NPC capital, exchange fees | Lifestyle purchases (depreciating luxury) and auction fees |
| Insurance payouts from NPC insurers | Legal fees; fines in Tape's six tiers (0.1% of net worth with a $1k floor and $5M cap, up to 15% with a $250k floor and no cap), split 85% sink, 10% victim fund, 5% whistleblower rewards. Victim payments are transfers, not sinks |
| Season rewards (non-cash where possible) | Cyber defense, compute rental from NPC clouds, data purchases from NPC |
| | Licenses, terminal subscriptions, IPO/underwriting fees |
| | 15% haircut on NPC business sales, liquidation penalties |
| | Interest on loans from NPC banks |

Player-to-player trades, P2P contracts, stealing and victim compensation are **transfers**: they move money, they don't create it.

### 8.2 The Stabilizer ("Game Central Bank")

A server-side controller measured every game day:
```
M(t)      = total player money + marked value of player assets   (per active player, P-weighted)
Net mint  = faucets − sinks
target    = population-adjusted growth path (≈ slight real growth per active player)
error e   = (M / M_target) − 1
```
Levers, adjusted slowly with a PI controller (`Δlever = Kp·e + Ki·∫e`), each clamped so players don't feel jerks:
1. NPC demand pools and equity drift (business income ±10%, index drift ±3%/yr)
2. Tax brackets (top bracket 25–45%)
3. Lifestyle and service prices (tracked as world inflation, so it reads as fiction, not a nerf)
4. Fees on high-volume trading
The stabilizer is **public as a fictional central bank** that publishes a "money supply report". Fewer players feel cheated if the mechanism is visible and diegetic.

### 8.3 Structural anti-inflation design

- **Logit market share + diminishing upgrades**: a business's income saturates; more money doesn't buy proportionally more income.
- **Progressive taxes and wealth upkeep**: top-tier assets (mansions, islands, sports franchises, megaprojects) carry large upkeep, insurance and staff costs. Being rich is expensive (ties to Tape's defense costs and Velvet's lifestyle).
- **Offline cap** (8–16 h) stops 24/7 idle printing.
- **NPC counterparty is not infinite**: NPC depth and NPC demand are finite per server, so a whale's profits are capped by the world's size, not exponential.
- **Leaderboard in real terms**: rank by **Net Worth Index** = net worth ÷ server money-supply index, plus a share-of-world-wealth view. Inflation can't pump rankings.
- **One persistent world** (Mathis, 2026-10-07): nothing resets, so the anti-inflation design can't lean on wipes. The extra rules for a world that runs forever are in §14.
- **Bankruptcy and liquidation** are real sinks and real comebacks: a busted player restarts with a protected minimum (a $5k "fresh start", at most once per 8 weeks, with a reputation hit).

### 8.4 Wealth progression targets (for balance runs)

Tuned so an engaged player (1–2 h/day active) roughly follows this curve from the day they join. The world never resets, so the curve runs past day 56; see §14.3 for the long tail.

| Day | Median engaged player | Top 1% | Top 0.01% |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $15k | $50k | $200k |
| 7 | $500k | $5M | $40M |
| 21 | $20M | $400M | $3B |
| 56 | $300M | $10B | $100B |

Growth must decelerate: daily growth ~60% on day 1 → ~5% by week 8 for the median. Tape's balance runs check this curve and flag any strategy that beats the top-1% path with no risk.

### 8.5 Starting fairness for late joiners

- Catch-up multiplier: new players get boosted business income (×1 + 0.5 · min(1, world_age_days / 56)) until they reach the world's median engaged net worth for their account age plus 14 days. It's capped at ×1.5 and ends after 30 days.
- Leagues by wealth bracket (Forge) so a new player competes with peers, while the global board stays visible for aspiration.

## 9. Exploits and how the engine shuts them down

| Exploit | Counter |
|---|---|
| Arbitrage loops (ETF vs basket, FX triangles, put-call parity, futures basis) | Parities hold by construction; NPC arbitrageurs close gaps within a tick; fees > residual |
| Alt-account funneling (feed money to a main) | P2P transfers taxed 2%, daily caps by account age, trade-at-off-market-price detection (price vs fair value > 10%), shared device/IP heuristics (Forge anti-cheat) |
| Wash trading / self-trading | Same beneficial owner can't match against itself; cross-alt matches flagged |
| Pump-and-dump, spoofing, layering | Surveillance score per player: large order cancels, buy-then-promote-then-sell patterns, news-outlet + position overlap. Feeds the regulator (Tape) |
| Buy business, upgrade, sell loop | 15% NPC haircut + upgrade costs are only 70% reflected in valuation |
| Leverage to the moon, get liquidated, restart fresh | Fresh start at most once per 8 weeks, reputation hit, debt to clearing house persists |
| Infinite offline income | Offline cap, decay without upkeep |
| Exploiting deterministic RNG (predicting prices) | Seed is server-secret and rotated; per-tick entropy is mixed in from a server commit; clients never run the sim |
| Timing news (reading the server ahead) | News is released to clients at the same tick it hits the engine; order batches close before news publish |
| Holding a monopoly in a city sector | Logit share means NPC entrants grow when margins are fat (entry rate ∝ sector profit margin); antitrust event when share > 60% (fine or forced sale) |
| Crashing a thin market to liquidate others | Impact caps, circuit breakers, liquidation uses fair value with a band, not the crashed print |
| Compute/data corners (buy all GPU-hours to starve rivals) | NPC cloud supply grows with price (elastic supply); position cap on compute futures; antitrust event |

## 10. Engine shape and MVP

### 10.1 Engine API (TypeScript, pure, deterministic, in `src/engine/` later)

```ts
createWorld(seed: string, config: WorldConfig): WorldState
step(state: WorldState, orders: Order[], actions: PlayerAction[]): { state: WorldState; fills: Fill[]; news: NewsEvent[]; metrics: EconomyMetrics }
quote(state, assetId): Quote                     // bid/ask/depth for the UI
valueBusiness(state, businessId): Valuation
portfolio(state, playerId): PortfolioView        // P&L, margin, exposure by factor
```
- Same code runs on the server (authoritative), in Tape's balance harness (thousands of simulated players), and in an offline single-player sandbox.
- Event-sourced: state = fold(seed, orders). Any day can be replayed to audit a disputed fill or a court case.

### 10.2 MVP market slice (suggested)

1. One region, one currency, one city.
2. 30 stocks across 8 sectors, one index, 5 crypto tokens, gold and oil.

7. Faucet/sink dashboard and the stabilizer in observe-only mode.
Everything else (bonds, forex, options, perps, IPOs, takeovers, multi-region macro) layers on without changing the core.

## 14. One persistent world (Mathis decision, 2026-10-07)

Mathis chose a single persistent world with a global leaderboard, country leaderboards and seasonal ladders inside the world. There are no resets and no visible shards. That removes the biggest anti-inflation tool (wiping the board), so the economy needs these rules instead.

### 14.1 Sizing the economy for one world

- Every pool scales with **activity-weighted players** `N_a` (Forge 02 §1), never sign-ups: NPC demand per city and sector, NPC market-maker depth, NPC compute demand, the startup pipeline, and the stabilizer's money target `M_target = m_per_active · N_a · real_growth_path`.
- **The map grows with the world.** At launch there are 5 regions and 15 cities. A new city opens in an existing region each time `N_a` grows by about 4,000, with fresh NPC demand, a new district map and new unique assets. Crowding stays roughly constant per city, and growth creates "new frontier" moments for early movers.
- **NPC world size:** NPC world GDP is set at about 50× total player net worth at launch and grows with `N_a`. That keeps players at a few percent of the world, so a whale can move a market but can't become the market.
- **Engine scaling (for Forge):** markets clear independently per asset, so batch auctions can be split across workers by asset group. Margin and mark-to-market can be split by player range, and the macro step is tiny and stays single-threaded. The `step()` cost grows roughly linearly with orders and open positions, so my earlier estimate of 150–300 ms at 4k concurrent players becomes about 40–75 ms per 1k concurrent per core-group. I'll replace that with a benchmark at first run.

### 14.2 Wealth gravity: keeping the top from running away forever

1. **Shrinking returns as a share of the world:** business income uses a logit share of finite demand, and market impact uses the square-root law. Above about 0.5% of world wealth, each extra dollar earns measurably less.
2. **Progressive upkeep and taxes:** these stay as in §8. The top income bracket (25–45%) only applies above $1B of yearly business profit.
3. **Concessions, not permanent ownership, for unique infrastructure:** the power utility, stock exchange, port terminal, chip fab and nuclear plant are 8-week regulated concessions, re-auctioned at each season boundary. The incumbent gets a 10% bid credit and keeps the built asset's book value as cash if outbid. Private islands and sports franchises stay owned outright but carry heavy upkeep. Fresh ones appear as new cities open.
4. **Dormant accounts:** after 30 real days without a login, a player leaves the active leaderboards but stays on the all-time board. Businesses run on managers at 50% income, and unique assets get no protection from antitrust or the takeover rules. After 90 days, unique assets go to an estate auction and the proceeds are paid to the owner as cash, so a returning player is never wiped. Market share and unique items flow back to active players.
5. **Real terms everywhere:** leaderboards rank by the Net Worth Index (§8.3), which is nominal net worth divided by the world money-supply index. Year-3 numbers stay comparable to launch numbers.

### 14.3 Long-tail progression target

| Days since joining | Median engaged | Top 1% | Top 0.01% |
|---|---|---|---|
| 56 | $300M | $10B | $100B |
| 180 | $2B | $60B | $400B |
| 365 | $6B | $120B | $800B |

Daily growth for top players should fall to about 0.5% by year 1, which makes catching up genuinely possible over months. Tape's balance runs should treat any strategy that holds above 2%/day at more than $100B, without risk, as an exploit.

### 14.4 Seasonal ladders inside the world (Ledger's addition)

- **Richest this season** = gain in real Net Worth Index from the season's start to its end. Money brought in by P2P transfers or contracts during the season doesn't count.
- **Fastest climber** = log growth of real net worth over the season, `ln(NW_end / max(NW_start, $100k))`. The $100k floor stops tiny accounts or fresh alts from posting huge percentage gains.
- **Country ladders** use the same metrics, filtered by the player's country.
- Ladder rewards are titles, cosmetics and Prestige, never cash. That keeps the seasonal reward faucet at zero for the money supply.
