## 0. Design principles

1. **Money has to feel like something.** A number going up is not a fantasy. Every rank of wealth changes what you see, where you live, what you can touch and who answers your calls. The player should *feel* the jump from $50k to $5M without reading a number.
2. **Your interface is your status.** The UI itself is a progression reward: you start on a cracked phone and end in a glass-walled trading floor above a city you partly own. Same layout, same muscle memory, different materials (§9.3). This is the signature idea of the game's look.
3. **Flex is earned, never bought with real money.** Prestige comes only from in-game money and in-game deeds. No real-money purchase can raise Prestige, net worth or rank (§12).
4. **Spending is a decision, not a tax.** Lifestyle items are mostly money sinks (Quant §8), so each one must give something real besides a picture: access, talent, credit, influence, convenience. Every benefit is small, capped, and never a cash faucet.
5. **Status is visible, and visibility is risk.** Big purchases hit the Wire. Prestige raises your heat with attackers and auditors (Tape). Showing off is a choice with a cost, like in life.
6. **Fast to learn, deep to master.** First dollar earned in under 60 seconds. The deep branches (derivatives, AI, cyber, court) open over days, each one introduced at the moment it becomes useful.
7. **Respect the player's time.** Real offline caps, no streak punishment, no fake urgency, notification caps (Forge §8.4). A 2-minute check-in must feel complete.
8. **Fictional world, real texture.** Every brand, city and institution is fictional (Kessler, Marchetti, Velour Maison, Port Avalon), but prices, depreciation and upkeep follow real-world shapes so the world feels grounded.

## 2. The player fantasy

**One line:** *Start with $5,000 and a phone. End with your name on a tower, a model everyone fears, and a seat at the top of the world board.*

There are three fantasies inside that line, and the game should let a player lean into any of them without losing:

| Fantasy | Plays like | Loves | Main boards |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Operator** | Builds and runs businesses, owns districts, vertical chains | City map, Tycoon screen, upgrades | Tycoon, Sector, World |
| **The Trader** | Lives in the terminal, reads news, trades every asset class | Charts, order book, tick pulse | Trader, Climber |
| **The Shadow** | Builds AI, steals data, hacks, litigates | Lab, Security Ops, court | Most Wanted, Top Labs, Defender |

And one fantasy that sits on top of all three: **The Name**. Lifestyle, Prestige, the Wire, the Gilt List (§8.3). This is where wealth turns into identity, and it is what makes people screenshot the game.

**Tone:** confident, sleek, a little ironic. Think a glossy finance magazine with a sense of humor, not a casino and not a cartoon. Headlines can be witty ("Local Coffee Cart Owner Buys Second Coffee Cart"), but money and losses are treated seriously. No real-world gambling cues: no slot-machine sounds, no spinning reels, no coin showers.

## 3. Progression arc: broke to empire

### 3.1 The seven ranks

Ranks are named stages of the player's life. They key off **net worth** and line up with Quant's business-tier unlocks and license tiers, Ledger's AI tiers, and the home and interface you get. Rank is never lost by dropping below a threshold (no demotion shame); your *current* standing shows in leagues and boards.

| Rank | Net worth | Business tier opens | License (Quant §3) | Typical home | Interface (§9.3) | AI tier (Ledger §8) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R0 **Broke** | start ($5k) | T1 Hustle | Cash account, Retail broker granted in onboarding | Rented room | Cracked phone | 0 Laptop script (teased) |
| R1 **Hustler** | $20k | T2 Small business | Retail broker | Studio | Phone, new | 0 |
| R2 **Owner** | $250k | T3 Company | Pro (forex, commodities, bonds, 2×) | Loft apartment | Laptop at a kitchen table | 1 Garage startup |
| R3 **Operator** | $5M | T4 Corporation | Derivatives (options, futures, up to 10×) | Penthouse | Three-screen desk | 2 Funded startup |
| R4 **Mogul** | $100M | T5 Conglomerate | Institutional | Mansion | Private office (walnut, brass) | 3 Scale-up lab |
| R5 **Titan** | $2B | T6 Megaproject | Institutional + Board seats | Estate | Glass trading floor over the city | 4–5 Frontier / Hegemon |
| R6 **Dynasty** | top 50 of the world board (Apex league) | Unique trophy auctions (island, franchise) | — | Private island or skyline tower | Island control room | — |

Licenses still cost money and need a clean record (Quant/Tape); the rank only makes them *available*.

### 3.2 When players get there

Interpolated from Quant's §8.4 curve (median engaged player, 1–2 h/day). Tape's harness should confirm.

| Rank | Median player reaches it | Top 1% reaches it |
|---|---|---|
| R1 Hustler ($20k) | day 1–2 | first session |
| R2 Owner ($250k) | ~day 5 | ~day 2 |

This gives everyone 4–5 rank-ups in a season, a fresh unlock every few days early on, and keeps Titan and Dynasty aspirational.

### 3.3 Milestones between ranks

Ranks are big; milestones fill the gaps so something good happens every session. Each milestone is a one-time moment with a cosmetic reward and a Wire mention for big ones. No cash rewards beyond Quant's onboarding budget.

| Milestone | Reward |
|---|---|
| First dollar earned | "First Dollar" framed bill on your wall (estate decor) |
| First profitable trade | Trade receipt card, shareable |
| Own 3 businesses | Business card design unlocked |
| **First $100k** | Watch box in your Vault |
| **First $1M ("Millionaire")** | Full-screen moment (§9.5), Wire headline, a champagne decor item |
| First property | House keys animation, map pin |
| First IPO of your company | Ringing-the-bell replay clip |
| First hack repelled | "Firewall" badge |
| Survive a crash with a positive week | "Iron Nerves" title |
| First court win | Gavel decor |
| **First $1B ("Billionaire")** | The biggest moment in the game short of season titles; the Gilt List cover (§8.3) |

### 3.4 The rank-up ceremony

Every rank-up plays a 4–6 second moment, skippable after 1 s, never during an open order countdown: the old interface material cracks or fades, the new one assembles (phone to laptop, laptop to desk), the new home is revealed through a window, and three unlock cards slide in ("T3 businesses", "Pro license", "Loft apartments"). It ends with one suggested next step, not a menu. A rank-up during market hours waits for the player to be idle for 10 s.

## 4. Onboarding and the first 10 minutes

### 4.1 Goals

- First dollar earned in under **60 s**.
- Teach exactly three verbs in the first session: **buy a business, trade, upgrade**. Everything else (property, AI, cyber, court, alliances) is visible but introduced later, when it pays.
- End the first session with a reason to come back: money earning while away, a rival, and a goal within reach.
- No tutorial wall, no forced text boxes longer than one sentence, everything skippable for veterans ("I've played before" skips to a free sandbox at minute 0).

Target metrics for Tape's funnel checks (targets, not measurements): 95% of new players buy a business, 80% make a trade, 60% reach $20k (R1) within their first two sessions.

### 4.2 Minute by minute

**0:00 — Cold open (8 s).** Black screen. A phone buzzes. A cracked lock screen lights up: *"Balance: $5,000.00. Rent due Friday."* A city skyline outside a small window (your starting city, Port Avalon by default). One button: **Start**.

**0:15 — Who are you.** Name, avatar (silhouette style, §9.6), starting city from the three cities of the FDL bloc. Then a *background* pick, which is pure flavor plus a tiny tilt, never more money:

| Background | Tilt |
|---|---|
| Dropout coder | Freelance dev studio and IT support shop highlighted, 10% off the first one |
| Ex-bank intern | Retail broker license comes with one free week of Terminal news |
| Street flipper | Sneaker resale and food truck highlighted, 10% off the first one |
| Barista with a plan | Coffee cart highlighted, 10% off the first one |

**0:40 — First business.** The City map zooms into one district with 4 suggested T1 spots glowing. The game suggests the cheapest one fitting your background (for example the **Coffee cart, $2,500**). One tap to buy. Keys animation.

**0:55 — First dollar.** The tick pulse appears (the 5 s ring, §9.5). On the next tick, the first earnings roll in with a soft number roll: *+$1.38*. The game says nothing; the number does the teaching. A tiny earnings counter now lives in the top bar.

**1:30 — First upgrade.** A customer queue forms on the cart illustration. Prompt: *"Your cart is turning people away."* Upgrade capacity for ~$400 (Quant's upgrade axes). Earnings visibly rise on the next tick. Lesson: money in, better money out.

**2:30 — First news, first trade.** A headline drops on the Wire: a real engine event from the world (not scripted fake news), picked as the most relevant one from the last game hour, for example *"Pallas Foods beats earnings."* The Retail broker license is granted as an onboarding reward (flag for Quant: one-time faucet, cost ~$250). The terminal opens in **Simple mode**: one chart, a Buy/Sell slider capped at 20% of cash for this first trade, and an order that fills on the next tick with the countdown ring. The game is honest: *"Prices can fall. That's the game."* If the trade loses, a short note shows why and how the stop-loss works.

**4:00 — The league.** Your league group appears: 50 players who joined around the same time, you're #41. One rival is highlighted: the closest player above you. Tap to see their public profile (businesses, titles, a trade receipt). This is the multiplayer hook in minute 4.

**5:00 — The look.** A free starter watch (Kessler Field, §6.3) in a gift box and one outfit pick. This opens the **Me** tab and shows your Prestige: *"Prestige 1. The top of your league has 14."* No spending required; the point is to show that status exists.

**6:00 — Player choice.** Three cards, pick one, no wrong answer:
- Buy a second business (spread the risk)
- Upgrade the first one twice (compound)
- Put $1,000 into an ETF (passive)
Each one plays out over the next ticks. This is the first real decision, and it's the player's.

**7:30 — The window to the future.** The camera pans across the room: a dusty laptop (*"The Lab opens at $100k"*), a locked drawer (*"Security. Your 72 h protection ends in 71:52, or the moment you attack someone"*; Tape's rookie zone then keeps the worst attackers off you until day 7 or $1M), a city poster with a mansion on a hill (*"Summit Estates. Someday."*). Each is tappable and shows one line plus the rank where it opens.

**8:30 — Goals.** Three starter goals appear in a small card: *Reach $20k*, *Own 2 businesses*, *Close a profitable trade*. Each goal is achievable in 1–2 sessions.

**9:30 — Leaving well.** *"Your cart keeps earning for 8 hours while you're away. Want a ping when you hit $20k?"* Only now, after value has been shown, does the game ask for push permission. Saying no is fine and never asked again for 7 days.

**10:00 — Free play.** No more prompts in this session unless the player opens Help.

### 4.3 Later onboarding: just-in-time introductions

Each deep system gets a 30–60 s introduction the first time it becomes useful, triggered by a real moment, not a calendar:

| System | Trigger | Introduction |
|---|---|---|
| Property | Net worth hits $50k and a studio listing in your district is below appraisal | "Buy the place you live in" mini-flow, mortgage explained in one card |
| Pro license | R2 rank-up | A guided first FX or bond trade |
| The Lab | Net worth $100k | The laptop wakes up: first model trained in 2 minutes on free public data |
| Security | 24 h before protection ends | A friendly pentest "drill" shows your weakest asset |
| Court | First filing, lawsuit, or theft event touching you | The docket explained with your own case |
| Alliances | Day 3, or when invited | Alliance HQ tour |
| Derivatives | R3 rank-up | Options explained with a payoff chart you can drag |

### 4.4 The returning session

Every return opens on **While You Were Away** (Forge §3.5): a single card stack, not a dashboard. Order: money earned (one big number), anything that needs action (margin call, lawsuit, contract signature, attack), then rivals (who passed you), then the Wire highlights. Swipe through in under 30 s, then land on Home. A player who opens the game for 2 minutes should be able to collect, react to one thing, and leave feeling caught up.

## 5. The lifestyle and status layer

### 5.1 What it is

Lifestyle is everything you buy for yourself rather than for return: homes you live in, cars, watches, yachts, jets, art, wine, fashion, experiences, philanthropy, and trophy assets. It has three jobs:

1. **Make wealth visible** to you and to others (Estate view, profile, Wire, Prestige board).
2. **Drain money from the economy** so the money supply stays healthy (Quant §8.1). Mass luxury is a sink by design.
3. **Give small, real advantages** so spending is a decision, not just a trophy (§7).

### 5.2 Item classes (aligned with Quant §3.10)

| Class | Examples | Price behavior | Prestige behavior |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mass** | New watches, new cars, standard yachts, fashion | Loses 10–60% on purchase, then slowly. Pure sink. | Fades with novelty (§7.2) |
| **Limited** | Numbered watches, limited supercars, gallery art, rare wine | Tracks Quant's collectible index, can appreciate | Holds, grows slightly with age |
| **Unique** | Masterworks, classic cars, private island, sports franchise | Auction only, one owner in the world | Highest, grows with age (heritage) |
| **Consumed** | Experiences, philanthropy | 100% sink on purchase | One-time burst (experiences) or Reputation (philanthropy) |

### 5.3 Where items live

- **Homes** are real estate (Quant §3.8): they sit in a district, have condition, upkeep and property tax, and count toward net worth at fair value. Owner-occupied homes don't earn rent but are your **Estate**, the background of your Home screen and the place where your collection is displayed.
- **Garage, marina, hangar, gallery, cellar, watch box**: capacity comes from your home. A studio fits 1 car; a mansion fits 12 cars, a gallery wing and a 2,000-bottle cellar; a yacht needs a marina berth (rented or owned). Capacity is a soft gate that makes homes matter.
- **The Vault** (§10.3): everything you own that isn't a business or a financial position, with its value, condition, insurance and Prestige.

### 5.4 Collections and sets

Items grouped into named sets give a Prestige bonus (+20% on the set's items) and a cosmetic title. Examples:

- **Harbor Weekend:** any yacht ≥ $12M + marina berth in Old Harbor + a Halden GT. Title: *Commodore*.
- **The Complication:** three Vael or Oriel complications. Title: *Horologist*.
- **Vetro Trilogy:** three classic Marchetti models. Title: *Collector*.
- **Patron:** a museum wing + three masterworks on loan to it. Title: *Patron of the Arts*.
- **Five Capitals:** a home in every region. Title: *Citizen of the World*.

Sets reward taste and breadth, not just the size of the cheque.

### 5.5 Real player-made goods

Lifestyle isn't only bought from NPCs. Players who own Quant's luxury businesses (watch manufacture, car dealership, fashion house, yacht builder) **make the items others buy**: a player watch manufacture can release a numbered edition under its own name ("Atelier Dubreuil, Edition 7 of 50"), and that edition becomes a Limited collectible in the world. This turns lifestyle into a real market with player brands, and it feeds Velour Maison and the Luxury sector exactly as Quant described.

### 6.7 Sink sanity check (for Quant and Tape)

A rough "typical spender" profile, to be replaced by harness runs: a median R4 Mogul at $100M with a mansion ($45M), a few mass cars (~$2.7M), an Aurel 80 ($12M), a light jet ($8M), three gallery pieces (~$3M) and a couture season holds about $71M in lifestyle at list price, most of it a home that still counts toward net worth. That's about $6M of instant purchase hit, plus roughly $4.3M per game year of upkeep and staff. At Quant's late-season median growth (~5% per real day), upkeep alone is small, so the real sink is the purchase hit plus novelty fade pushing players to refresh mass items. If Quant's stabilizer needs more drain, I'd raise upkeep on yachts, jets and staff first and leave list prices alone, because price is what makes the flex feel real. If the harness shows lifestyle costs above ~25% of an R4 player's income, lower upkeep first.

## 7. What spending gives besides flex

### 7.1 Three social stats

| Stat | Comes from | Goes down from | Shown |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Prestige (PP)** | Owning and displaying lifestyle items, titles, experiences | Selling items, letting condition drop, novelty fade | Public, Prestige board, profile |
| **Reputation (0–100)** | Philanthropy, honest filings, contracts honored, clean lab, court wins as a victim | Convictions, broken contracts, failed filings, rug pulls | Public, profile badge |
| **Notoriety (0–100)** | Successful ops, Most Wanted rank (Tape) | Convictions decay it into plain bad Reputation | Opt-in public (Tape's rule) |

This answers Ledger's morality question: being clean and being feared are separate axes, and a player can be a respected philanthropist with a secret Notoriety score. Reputation gates a few honest-world doors (§7.3); Notoriety unlocks underworld contacts (Tape's informants and black-market data, if Tape and Ledger want them).

### 7.2 Prestige formula

```
PP_item     = round( sqrt(price_at_purchase / 1,000) × rarity_mult × condition × age_factor )
rarity_mult = Mass 1 · Limited 2 · Unique 4
age_factor  = Mass / fashion:  1.0 → 0.5 linearly over 1 game year (novelty fades)
              Limited / Unique: 1.0 → 1.25 linearly over 2 game years (heritage grows)
Category sum: items in the same category sorted by PP, weights 1, 0.5, 0.25, 0.125, …
Prestige    = Σ categories + set bonuses (+20% on set items) + titles + active experience bursts
```

Why this shape: the square root keeps Prestige from simply mirroring net worth (a $1B item gives ~32× the PP of a $1M one, not 1,000×), the category weights reward breadth over owning 40 identical supercars, and the age factors make mass luxury a recurring sink while real collectibles reward patience.

Benchmarks: a new player has ~1 PP, a typical R2 Owner ~40, an R3 Operator ~250, an R4 Mogul ~1,000, an R5 Titan ~5,000, and Dynasty players 15,000+.

### 7.3 Access (clubs and invitations)

Clubs are membership tiers you qualify for with Prestige *and* Reputation, then pay dues (a sink). They open doors, not money:

| Club | Needs | Dues / game yr | Opens |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Harbor Club** | 250 PP, Reputation ≥ 40 | $150,000 | Private deal room listings (Quant §3.9) 1 game day before the public; a members' channel |
| **Glasshill Circle** | 1,000 PP, Reputation ≥ 50 | $2,000,000 | Seat at the quarterly central bank Gala: the Rumor feed's accuracy goes from 40–70% to 45–75% for that game week (Quant §6.2) |
| **Summit Society** | 5,000 PP, Reputation ≥ 60 | $25,000,000 | Pre-auction viewing of Unique items (bid 1 game hour early), board-seat invitations on NPC companies |
| **The Back Room** | Notoriety ≥ 50 (any Prestige) | $1,000,000 | Black-market data and fixers (Tape and Ledger to define), never shown on your public profile |

Information edges stay small and noisy so clubs never become guaranteed profit (Tape's harness should check).

### 7.4 Hosting

A mansion or better lets you **host**. An event is a time-boxed private room for up to 8 (dinner), 30 (party) or 100 (gala) invited players for one game day. During it: contract fees between attendees are waived, a temporary alliance-style chat exists, and every attendee gets a small Reputation bump if the host's Reputation is ≥ 50. Hosting costs money (catering, staff) and shows on the Wire. It's the social engine for deals, alliances and drama.

### 7.5 Convenience and reach

- **Jets: fly-in.** Opening a business or buying property in another region normally has a 1 game day setup delay (flag for Quant: propose this delay); a jet removes it. Light jet for your home bloc, long-range for any region.
- **Chief of Staff (mansion+).** Queues up to 3 actions to run at the next market open while you're offline (an upgrade, a limit order, an upkeep payment). Convenience only; Quant's offline earning cap is unchanged.
- **Talent.** Prestige raises the quality of hiring pools for business managers and AI researchers (Ledger §3.3): +1 candidate per pool per 1,000 PP, capped at +3, and +10% poach resistance at Titan. People want to work for a name.

### 7.6 Credit and security

- **Collateral.** Limited and Unique items and homes can back loans at an NPC bank: homes at Quant's mortgage LTVs, Unique items at 50%, Limited at 30%, Mass at 0%. A crash in the collectible index can trigger margin calls on art (good drama).
- **Physical security.** Homes from penthouse up have a security wing (optional upgrade, sink) that raises defense against Tape's *physical* espionage and theft ops only, never cyber. Wealth buys walls, not invisibility.

### 7.7 Influence

Your public statements on the Wire reach more people with Prestige: `reach_weight = 1 + 0.25 × log10(1 + PP)`, capped at 2×. That changes how widely your post is shown and, through Quant §6.3, how much it nudges sentiment. Lying is still manipulation (Tape).

### 7.8 The cost of being seen

- Lifestyle purchases above $10M post automatically to the Wire ("Mogul *K. Reyes* buys a Gigayacht").
- Prestige adds heat (§1): it raises your exposure as a target only, never breach or detection odds. It also slightly raises tax-audit odds for very high PP with low declared income (Tape's regulator).
- Thieves target collections: Quant's theft events weight by collection value; Tape's insurance covers them.
- A player can stay "quiet money": skip lifestyle entirely, lose the benefits in §7.3–7.7, and keep a lower profile. That's a valid strategy and should stay one.

## 8. Showing status

### 8.1 The Estate view

The Home screen background is your actual home, rendered as a stylized illustration at the time of day of your city, with your collection placed in it: cars in the drive, art on the walls, the yacht at the dock below if you have a marina. Tapping an item opens it in the Vault. Other players can **visit** your estate (read-only) from your profile. This is the screenshot moment.

### 8.2 Profile card

Name, rank badge, title of choice, Prestige, Reputation, opt-in Notoriety, top 3 displayed items, season history, and pinned trade receipts. A compact version appears everywhere your name does (leaderboards, Wire, court docket, chat).

### 8.3 The Gilt List

Every game quarter (≈ 3.7 real days), the world publishes **The Gilt List**, a fictional magazine issue: the top 100 by net worth with photos of their estates, biggest climbers, "Most Flamboyant" (Prestige), "Most Generous" (Reputation), and one cover star. Being on the cover is a shareable card. It reuses Forge's Prestige board and Quant's filings, so it's mostly a design job.

### 8.4 Season end: what you keep

Season worlds reset (Forge §2). Lifestyle items are wealth, so they reset too. What carries over is memory, never value:
- A **season portrait**: a rendered picture of your estate at Settlement, kept in your Hall of Fame.
- One **heirloom** of your choice: a cosmetic copy of one item (your first watch, the island) that appears in every future home, with no monetary value or Prestige.
- Titles from sets, milestones and boards.

## 9. Visual direction (proposal; Mathis decides)

This is one of the big calls Ledger takes to Mathis. Three directions, with my recommendation.

### 9.1 Options

| | **A. Ink & Brass** (recommended) | **B. Glass Bank** | **C. Neon Ledger** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feel | Night-time private bank meets trading terminal. Deep ink, warm brass, editorial serif headlines, mono numbers. | Bright, frosted glass, white space, soft shadows. Premium fintech. | Cyberpunk: black, neon cyan and magenta, glitch effects. |
| Strength | Reads as money and power, stays readable for hours of charts, makes luxury items look expensive, ages well | Friendly, clean on mobile, easy to read | Instantly fits the hacking and AI layer |
| Risk | Can feel heavy if overdone | Generic, looks like a real bank app, weak at night | Tiring for long sessions, makes luxury look cheap, very common in games |

**Why A:** the game is about *wealth* first and crime second. Ink & Brass makes a watch or a mansion feel valuable, and charts sit beautifully on dark surfaces. The Shadow side (Lab, Security Ops) gets its own sub-palette inside A (cold steel and a single signal red), so the hacking layer feels dangerous without the whole game going neon.

### 9.2 Ink & Brass in one paragraph

Dark surfaces with subtle depth (no pure black), one warm metal accent used sparingly for wealth moments, a high-contrast serif for big headlines and money moments, a clean sans for UI, and a monospaced face with tabular figures for every number that changes. Gains and losses use teal and coral (not pure green and red) plus arrows and signs, so they work for color-blind players. Illustrations are flat-ish, architectural, lit like a magazine shoot at dusk.

### 9.3 The interface as status (signature idea)

### 9.5 Motion and sound

- **The tick pulse.** A thin ring in the top bar fills over 5 s and closes on each tick (Forge's countdown). On close: prices that changed flash their gain/loss tint for 400 ms, balances roll digits over 120 ms. Pending orders show the same ring. This is the heartbeat of the game and the one animation players see thousands of times, so it stays subtle.
- **Moments budget.** Only a few things get full-screen motion: rank-ups (4–6 s), first $1M and $1B (6 s), Unique item purchases (2.4 s reveal), court verdicts (3 s), being hacked (a 1.5 s red edge pulse, then straight to the facts). Everything is skippable after 1 s.
- **Reduced motion.** Respects the OS setting: no rolls, no flashes, instant transitions, the tick ring becomes a static countdown number.
- **Sound.** Off by default on phones, on by default on desktop with a master slider. A soft bell for the market open and close, a muted tick on order fills, a low thud when attacked. No coin, slot or casino sounds anywhere.
- **Haptics (mobile).** Light tap on order fill, medium on rank-up, a double pulse on an attack alert.

### 9.6 Illustration and avatars

- Architectural illustration for homes, districts and businesses: clean shapes, dusk lighting, a few figures as silhouettes.
- Avatars are stylized silhouettes and portrait cards (no realistic faces at launch), customizable with outfit, watch and pose. This keeps art scope sane and avoids uncanny faces. Open question for Mathis (§16).
- Item renders: each lifestyle item gets one hero image on a dark studio backdrop, same lighting across all items, so the Vault looks like a catalog.

## 11. Session rhythm and retention

### 11.1 Three session lengths

| Session | Length | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Check-in | ~2 min | While You Were Away, collect, react to one alert, queue one action |
| Session | ~15 min | Upgrade, a few trades, one property or business move, check the league |
| Deep session | ~1 h (one market day) | Trade the open and close auctions, run a Lab training plan, plan an op, host or attend an event |

### 11.2 Natural rhythms (all from Quant's clock)

- **Every 5 s:** the tick.
- **Every real hour:** a market day with open and close auctions; a good moment for a deep session.
- **Every ~3 real days:** a game quarter: earnings season, central-bank meetings, the Gilt List.
- **Every 7 real days:** league week with promotion and relegation.
- **Every 8 weeks:** season finale and Settlement.

### 11.3 Ethics guardrails

- No daily-login streaks that punish missing a day. A "comeback" bonus is fine, a broken streak is not.
- No timers sold for real money, no loot boxes, no random paid rewards, nothing that looks like gambling (also an anti-job for the whole team).
- Offline earnings cap is explained in plain words up front so nobody feels robbed.
- Notifications respect Forge's cap (~5/day) and quiet hours by local time.
- The game never frames anything as real financial advice; the terminal footer reads "Simulated market. Not financial advice." in small type.

## 13. Accessibility and localization

- Color is never the only signal: gains and losses also use arrows and signs; attack states also use icons and text.
- Contrast: body text ≥ 4.5:1 on all surfaces; brass is never used for body text.
- Text scales to 200% without breaking the terminal (panels stack instead of overlapping).
- Full keyboard play on desktop, with the command palette as the fast path; screen-reader labels on every number ("Corvo Motors, 42.10 dollars, up 1.2 percent").
- Reduced motion respected everywhere (§9.5).
- Localization ready from day one: English first; French is an obvious second language given the team (Mathis to confirm). Number and date formats follow the player's locale; the fictional currency symbol stays $.
