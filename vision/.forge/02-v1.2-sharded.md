# 02 — Multiplayer Systems

Owner: Forge (app + platform). Status: draft v1.2, 2026-10-07 (synced with RESOLVED-NUMBERS.md incl. C3a, and teammates' answers).
Scope: how the world is hosted and ticked, worlds and seasons, accounts, leaderboards and leagues, alliances, player-to-player trading and contracts, social, anti-cheat, the web + mobile stack, live ops, and what an MVP vs the full build looks like technically.

Builds on Quant's `01-markets-economy.md` (5 s tick, per-tick batch auction, deterministic seeded engine, event sourcing, seasons of 8 weeks, Net Worth Index). Where I give a number, it is a design target to be validated by Tape's load and balance runs, not a measurement.

---

## 0. Design principles

1. **Server is the only truth.** Clients never run the authoritative sim, never hold the seed, never compute their own fills or balances. A client is a window plus an order pad.
2. **Ticks, not twitch.** Everything that matters resolves on the 5 s world tick. Your ping, device and connection never decide a trade, a hack or a race. A phone on 4G and a desktop on fiber are equal.
3. **Async-first multiplayer.** Most players play 15–60 minutes a day across timezones. Every interaction between players must work when the other side is offline: contracts with deadlines, standing orders, AIs that act for you, defenses that run without you.
4. **Persistent consequence, seasonal competition.** Your identity, reputation, titles and cosmetics last forever. Your competitive fortune resets each season so new players always have a real shot.
5. **Everything is a ledger entry.** Every cent moves through one double-entry ledger with a reason code. That single rule powers anti-cheat, court evidence, rollbacks, the stabilizer's faucet/sink metrics and customer support.
6. **Social pressure is the content.** The best moments come from other people: a rival's IPO you can short, an alliance betrayal, a whale's public filing, a court verdict. Systems should surface other players constantly.
7. **One codebase, three screens.** Desktop web, mobile web/PWA, and native wrappers share one Next.js app and one TypeScript engine package.

Size NPC demand pools and NPC depth off **active players with a 7-day decay**, not registrations, so a world that empties out doesn't become a bonanza for the remaining whales:
```
A(t)           = Σ_players  activity_weight_p,   activity_weight = e^(−days_since_last_active / 7)
NPC_scale(t)   = clamp(A(t) / 8,000, 0.25, 1.5)
demand_pool    = base_pool · NPC_scale(t)     (smoothed with a 3-game-day EMA)
```
MVP alpha world: 500 registered, ~150 CCU. Pools should work from 100 to 30,000 players via `NPC_scale`.

## 2. World topology: worlds, seasons, legacy

### 2.1 Worlds (shards)

A **world** is one economy: one engine instance, one set of markets, cities, NPC companies, one court, one leaderboard. Players only trade, hack and sue inside their own world.

Why shard instead of one global economy:
- Quant's anti-inflation design needs a **finite** NPC world so a whale's profit is capped by the world's size. A single global economy with 500k players has either infinite NPC depth (inflation) or impossible congestion.
- A 20k-player world is small enough that the top 100 are **known names**. Rivalry needs recognition. In a million-player economy nobody knows who anyone is.
- One engine process per world keeps the sim single-writer and deterministic (no distributed consensus).
- Worlds can be themed and timed (regional prime time, ruleset variants).

What crosses worlds:
- Account, profile, cosmetics, titles, trophies, friends, DMs.
- **Global leaderboard** ranked by Net Worth Index (net worth ÷ that world's money-supply index), so worlds compare fairly.
- Cross-world events (§6.6 Champions Cup).
- Nothing economic. No money, items, data or models move between worlds (prevents laundering via a weak world).

World naming: diegetic exchange cities, e.g. "Meridian-EU-01", "Glasshill-NA-02" (matches Quant's fictional city names).

**Regions and prime time.** Batching makes latency irrelevant for fairness, but timezone still matters: earnings calls, central-bank meetings and crashes should land when the world's players are awake. Each world has a home region (EU, NA, APAC) and its scheduled big events (Quant's earnings seasons, central-bank meetings, season finale) are placed in that region's 18:00–23:00 window. Players can join any world; the region only sets the event clock. Server hosting region matches the home region for snappier UI, not for fairness.

### 2.2 Season lifecycle (8 weeks, from Quant's time model)

| Phase | Real time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-season | 3 days before | World page opens, sign-ups, alliance registration, "draft" of starting city, trailer of the season's macro theme (e.g. "The Rate Shock Season") |
| Opening Bell | Day 1 | Everyone starts at $5k. New players get 72 real hours of protection (no hacks or lawsuits against them), which ends early the moment they attack anyone; after that a rookie zone applies until day 7 or $1M net worth, whichever comes first (Tape 03 §15) |
| Early game | Days 1–14 | Businesses, retail broker license, first alliances. Leagues placed after day 3 |
| Mid game | Days 15–42 | Derivatives, IPOs, takeovers, cyber layer fully open, alliance wars. Quant's crisis odds peak here |
| Endgame | Days 43–52 | Last licenses, megaprojects, sports franchises auctioned |
| Closing Bell week | Days 53–56 | No new players, leaderboard snapshot rules apply (§6.4), final macro event, public "Closing Bell" livestream-style recap screen |
| Settlement | Day 57 | World freezes, final ranks computed, rewards granted, Hall of Fame entries written, world archived read-only (players can browse their final empire and replays forever) |
| Off-season | 3–7 days | Flash worlds (§2.4), cosmetic shop rotation if any, patch deploys |

Late joiners: Quant's catch-up multiplier applies. A world closes to new joins on day 42; joiners after that go to the next world or a flash world.

### 2.3 Legacy world (persistent)

Quant's open question #2. My technical recommendation: **season worlds are the main game; one persistent Legacy world per region opens in phase 3**.

- Legacy world never resets. Growth is slower (NPC_scale 0.6, higher upkeep, stronger stabilizer gains).
- What you can carry in: nothing economic. You start a legacy character at $5k like everyone, but with your account titles.
- Why: persistent worlds always end up frozen at the top. Seasons keep the leaderboard alive. A legacy world serves players who love building and hate losing their empire, without poisoning competition.
- Technical cost: same engine; the difference is config plus long-term state hygiene (archival of dead players' assets to NPC every 30 real days of inactivity, see §3.7).

### 2.4 Flash worlds (new idea)

Short worlds with modified rules, run during off-seasons and on weekends:
- **24h Speedrun:** tick compressed 4× (market day = 15 real minutes), 200 players, starts at $5k, ends after 24 h.
- **Crash Course:** world starts one game-week before a scripted crash. Who survives?
- **No-Hack Pure Markets:** cyber layer off, for traders who hate the conflict layer.
- **Hacker's Paradise:** defenses at 50%, court at 50% — chaos mode.
Rewards are cosmetic and titles only. Flash worlds are cheap tests for new rules before they hit season worlds.

## 3. Simulation and networking architecture

### 3.1 Processes

```
                ┌────────────────────────────────────┐
 Browser/PWA ──►│  Edge: Next.js app (pages, API,    │──► Postgres (accounts, social,
 Capacitor app  │  auth, REST for non-realtime)      │     contracts, archive)
                └──────────────┬─────────────────────┘
                               │ WebSocket (realtime)
                ┌──────────────▼─────────────────────┐
                │  Gateway (stateless, N instances)   │  auth check, rate limit,
                │  fan-out of tick deltas             │  subscriptions, presence
                └──────────────┬─────────────────────┘
                               │ Redis streams / pub-sub
                ┌──────────────▼─────────────────────┐
                │  World process (1 per world)        │  single writer, runs engine.step
                │  intake queue → validate → step     │  every 5 s, owns world state
                └───┬──────────────────────┬─────────┘
                    │                      │
              Event log (append)      Snapshots (every game day)
              Postgres partitioned    object storage (S3-compatible)
```

- **Next.js** serves the app, auth, and everything that isn't real-time: profile, contract drafting, alliance admin, archives, leaderboards history.
- **Gateway** holds WebSocket connections (target ~10k sockets per instance), authenticates, applies rate limits, and forwards player intents to the right world's intake queue. It subscribes to the world's tick output and sends each client only what it subscribed to.
- **World process** is a long-running Node.js (or Bun) process that owns the world state in memory and runs Quant's `step()` every tick. Exactly one writer per world. A hot standby follows the event log and can take over.
- Next.js on serverless cannot hold a 5 s game loop or WebSockets reliably; the world and gateway are separate long-lived services.

### 3.2 The tick pipeline (every 5 s)

| Step | Budget | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Intake close | t = 0 | Batch of intents for tick N is sealed. Late intents roll into N+1 (client is told which tick it landed in) |
| 2. Validate | 100 ms | Auth, ownership, balance/margin pre-check, rate limits, license gates, frozen-asset checks (court), anti-cheat flags |
| 3. AI phase | 600 ms | Player AIs (trading, cyber, defense) evaluate and emit intents into the same batch (§4) |
| 4. Engine step | 1,000 ms (Quant's unbenchmarked worst case: 150–300 ms, with AI evaluation in step 3 and businesses updating once per game hour) | `step(state, orders, actions)` → fills, price updates, business income, hacks resolved, news |
| 5. Ledger commit | 300 ms | Append events + ledger entries to Postgres in one transaction. Tick N is final only after commit |
| 6. Broadcast | 300 ms | Diff of public state + per-player private deltas to gateways → clients |
| Slack | ~2.7 s | Headroom for spikes (crash days, season finale) |

If a step overruns, the next tick waits (world time slows slightly) rather than skipping. A tick never runs partially. Tape's load test target: 4,000 CCU and 20,000 AI agents with p99 tick work < 2.5 s.

News and fills for tick N publish together at step 6, after the batch for N+1 is already open, so no client can react to news inside the tick that produced it (matches Quant §9).

### 3.3 Determinism and event sourcing

- World state = fold(seed, config, all sealed intent batches, server entropy commits). Quant's engine is pure; the world process adds only I/O.
- **Event log:** one row per sealed batch plus one row per resulting event (fill, transfer, hack, verdict). Partitioned by world and game week.
- **Snapshots:** full state serialized every game day (hourly real time) and at season phase changes, stored in object storage. Recovery = latest snapshot + replay of remaining batches (target < 60 s to recover a crashed world).
- **Replay API:** any tick range can be replayed in a sandbox to reproduce a disputed fill, a hack, or a bug. Court cases (Tape) and support tickets cite tick numbers.
- **Versioning:** each batch records the engine version. A deploy mid-season replays the last game day on both versions in CI to check no divergence beyond intended changes.
- **Secret seed:** per-world seed is server-only and per-tick entropy is mixed from a commit-reveal (hash published at tick N, value revealed at N+1000). Lets us prove after the fact that we didn't rig an outcome.

### 3.4 What clients receive

Interest management keeps bandwidth tiny. Clients subscribe to channels:

| Channel | Content | Size per tick (est.) |
|---|---|---|
| `ticker:watchlist` | price, change, volume for ≤ 50 symbols | ~1.5 KB |
| `book:{symbol}` | top 10 levels (only for the open trade screen) | ~0.5 KB |
| `me` | balances, positions, fills, margin, alerts, attacks on me | ~0.5 KB (often 0) |
| `city:{id}` | demand, rents, business news for open city view | ~1 KB on change |
| `news` | headlines for the world | ~0.3 KB avg |
| `alliance:{id}` | treasury, chat, ops board | on change |
| `board:top` | top 100 movers | every game hour only |

At ~4 KB per tick → under 1 KB/s per active client. Fine for mobile data. Binary encoding (MessagePack) and delta compression keep it small; JSON in dev.

**Client prediction:** none for money. Orders show as "pending tick N" with a countdown ring (also a nice design moment for Velvet: the tick pulse). UI optimism only for cosmetic things (moving a widget, renaming a business).

**Reconnects:** the client sends its last seen tick; the gateway replays missed `me` deltas (up to 1 game day) or sends a fresh snapshot.

### 3.5 Offline play

- Standing orders (limit, stop, take-profit, trailing) live server-side and fire while you sleep.
- Businesses earn up to the offline cap (Quant: 8 h, 16 h with a manager).
- Your AIs keep running inside their mandate (§4) and your cyber defense always runs.
- On return: a **"While You Were Away" report** (what earned, what filled, who attacked you, lawsuits filed, alliance events). This is the core re-engagement screen.
- Push notifications for things that need you (§8.4), all opt-in and throttled.

### 3.6 Read models

The world process emits events; projectors build read models in Postgres/Redis for the app: leaderboards, player profiles, public filings, court docket, market history (OHLC candles at 1 tick, 1 game hour, 1 game day), news archive. The app never queries the world process for history.

### 3.7 State hygiene in long worlds

- Inactive players (no login for 14 real days) go **dormant**: businesses run at 50% with no upkeep penalty, they can't be targeted by hacks (nothing to steal from a ghost), positions stay.
- After 30 days dormant in the Legacy world, their assets are auctioned to NPC/players and they keep cash in a frozen account restored on return. Stops the world filling with ghost-owned districts.

## 4. AI agents on the server (touches Ledger 05)

Player AIs are the game's most expensive and most exploitable system. Rules:

1. **No user code runs on our servers.** Player "AIs" are built from in-game components: a model tier (from Ledger's tech tree), a data set, and a **strategy graph** made of blocks (signals, filters, sizing rules, execution algos, risk limits). Think visual rule builder plus learned parameters, not a Python sandbox. This avoids sandbox escapes and keeps CPU predictable.
2. **Same API as humans.** AIs submit intents into the same batch through the same validation. No special order types, no early data.
3. **Per-tick budget (resolved, RESOLVED-NUMBERS.md):** each AI gets an evaluation budget in decision units (1 DU = evaluate one signal on one symbol). Hard caps: **2,000 DU/tick per player** and **3,000,000 DU/tick per world**. If a world goes over, every AI scales down proportionally (diegetic "compute shortage", which also raises Quant's compute price).

   | AI tier (Ledger 05 §8) | Cadence | Base DU per evaluation | Effective DU/tick |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | T0 Laptop script | every 6 ticks | 30 | 5 |
   | T1 Garage startup | every 6 ticks | 90 | 15 |
   | T2 Funded startup | every 3 ticks | 300 | 100 |
   | T3 Scale-up lab | every 2 ticks | 800 | 400 |
   | T4 Frontier lab | every tick | 1,500 | 1,500 |
   | T5 Hegemon | every tick | 2,000 | 2,000 |

   Compute raises the cap, relative to tier (RESOLVED-NUMBERS C3a): `DU_cap(player) = min(2000, base_tier · (1 + 0.25·min(1, compute_owned / tier_run_midpoint)))`, so owned or reserved compute adds at most +25% over the tier's base, and the worst case is ~2.9M DU/tick for 20k agents, inside the world cap. `compute_owned` counts only owned GPUs and reserved contracts; hijacked compute (Tape 03 §3) never raises a DU cap. DU is a server CPU cap (mine); in-game compute in PFLOP-days/GPU-hours is a priced commodity (Quant/Ledger). The two are never conflated. Cadences are staggered by player id so T0–T3 agents don't all fire on the same tick.
4. **AI quality = information, not speed.** A better model narrows Quant's σ on hidden variables. It never gets earlier ticks.
5. **Mandates:** you set limits your AI must obey (max position, max drawdown, allowed assets, legal-risk tolerance). An AI that breaks the law under your mandate is still your legal problem (Tape).
6. **Distillation is an event, not a file copy.** A stolen model is represented as parameters in the world state; distilling copies a fraction of quality minus noise. Its provenance stays traceable in the event log (court evidence, watermarking idea for Ledger/Tape: models carry a hidden fingerprint that survives distillation with probability p).
7. **Scale estimate:** 8,000 WAU × ~2.5 AIs each = 20,000 agents. At the expected tier mix (T0 40%, T1 30%, T2 18%, T3 8%, T4 3%, T5 1%) the average is ~121 effective DU/tick per agent, so ~2.43M DU/tick per world, inside the 3.0M cap. That is ~4M signal evaluations per second, so the AI phase runs on a worker-thread pool (one worker per core, agents sharded by id) to fit the 600 ms budget. This is an estimate; Tape's load test replaces it with a measured number before Phase 3.

## 5. Accounts and identity

### 5.1 Sign-in

- **Guest first:** play the first session with no account (device-bound guest). Required to join a season world after 30 minutes or before any P2P trade. Guests can't send money or contracts (anti-alt).
- **Upgrade paths:** email magic link, passkeys, Google, Apple, Discord. Apple sign-in is required on iOS if we offer other social logins in the native app (App Store rule), so we ship it from the start.
- **Auth library:** Better Auth or Auth.js in the Next.js app, sessions in Postgres, short-lived signed tokens for the WebSocket gateway.
- **2FA** (TOTP or passkey) required for top-1% wealth players and alliance leaders, to protect them from real account theft (very different from in-game hacking, and we must make that distinction loud in the UI).

### 5.2 Identity layers

| Layer | Lifetime | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| Account | forever | login, email, region, age band, purchases, bans |
| Profile | forever | handle (unique, changeable once per season), avatar, titles, trophies, Hall of Fame, cosmetics, reputation history |
| Season character | one season | money, assets, AIs, legal record, alliance, league |

- **Display identity:** handle plus optional company name ("vega · Northline Capital"). Legal names never shown.
- **Reputation** (persistent, cross-season): a public record of seasons played, final ranks, convictions (Tape), contracts honored vs defaulted. Contract counterparties see it before signing. This is the main deterrent against scamming in P2P (more on that §7).

### 5.3 Compliance basics

- Age: 13+ globally, 16+ in EU countries that require it for data consent (or parental consent flow). Since the game is about money, markets and hacking, a 16+ rating is simpler; open question for Mathis.
- GDPR: data export and account deletion from settings. Deleting an account anonymizes the season character ("Former player #8812") so the event log and other players' histories stay consistent.
- Clear in-game disclaimers: fictional markets, no real money in or out, nothing is financial advice. No cash-out path ever exists, which also keeps us out of gambling law.

## 6. Leaderboards and leagues

### 6.1 Ranking metric

Primary: **Net Worth Index (NWI)** from Quant = net worth ÷ world money-supply index. Net worth uses fair-value marks (not last print) for illiquid assets so nobody can pump their rank by trading a thin asset with an alt.

Snapshot cadence: top 100 live (every game hour); full board every game day; league tables every real day at a fixed time.

### 6.2 Boards (many ways to be "the best")

| Board | Ranks by | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Global (cross-world) | NWI | Aspiration, the headline board |
| World | NWI | The real rivalry |
| League | NWI growth this league week | Peers compete; new players matter |
| Trader | Season Sharpe ratio with min. volume | Rewards skill, not size |
| Tycoon | Business profit per game year | Builders' board |
| Climber | Biggest % gain over 7 days | Comebacks, keeps mid-table engaged |
| Alliance | Sum of member NWI (top 20 members) | Team play |
| Prestige | Lifestyle/collection score (Velvet) | Flex board |
| Most Wanted | Successful hacks on unique, unlinked victims, minus convictions | Villain board: opt-in, cosmetic rewards only (Tape 03) |
| Defender | Attacks repelled, value protected | Rich players get a board for being hard to crack |
| Sector boards | Profit per sector | Lots of smaller crowns |

Each board crowns season titles ("Season 3 Trader Champion"). Many boards means many players can win something.

### 6.3 Leagues

Weekly leagues inside a world, placed by NWI bracket after day 3:

| League | Rough bracket (day 7 numbers from Quant) | Group size |
|---|---|---|
| Penny | bottom 30% | 50 |
| Retail | 30–60% | 50 |
| Pro | 60–85% | 50 |
| Institutional | 85–97% | 50 |
| Titan | top 3% | 30 |
| Apex | top 50 in world | 1 group |

- Each league week (7 real days), top 10 of a group promote, bottom 10 relegate. League score = % NWI growth that week, so a $100k player can beat a $10M player in their own group by playing well.
- Rewards are cosmetic plus a small non-cash perk (e.g. a week of +1 news-feed slot), never cash (no faucet).
- **Anti-sandbagging:** placement is by NWI, not chosen. Deliberately losing money to drop a league is self-defeating because league rewards are worth less than the lost wealth; Tape should still check for alts dumping into a main to rise.
- Groups are formed with similar join dates and a mix of timezones.

### 6.4 Season-end rules

- Final rank = NWI at Settlement, but averaged over the last 3 game days (prevents a one-tick pump on the final second, or a last-second leveraged moonshot).
- Leverage cap halves in Closing Bell week.
- Positions in player-issued assets (own IPO, own token) are marked at the lower of fair value and 3-day VWAP.

### 6.5 Hall of Fame and legacy

- Permanent per-season records: champions of every board, biggest single trade, biggest crash survived, most notorious hack, longest-held alliance.
- **Dynasty perk:** a small permanent cosmetic/convenience perk per season completed (e.g. extra watchlist slots, a unique office skin), never wealth. Quant suggested a small "legacy" perk; I recommend it be non-economic to keep fresh starts fair.

### 6.6 Champions Cup (new idea)

Top 10 of each world are invited to a **cross-world flash world** at season end: 48 hours, identical starting conditions, broadcast-style spectator page. Winner gets the rarest title. Gives worlds a reason to care about each other.

## 7. Alliances and corporations

### 7.1 Structure

| Tier | Members | Unlocked by |
|---|---|---|
| Partnership | 2–5 | Free |
| Firm | up to 15 | $5M charter fee (sink) + HQ office |
| Corporation | up to 40 | $250M + HQ tower in a city |
| Conglomerate | up to 40 + 3 subsidiary firms | $5B, late season |

Caps keep any group from dominating a 20k world (40 members × 4 entities ≈ 1% of players at most).

### 7.2 What an alliance can do

- **Treasury:** shared account funded by voluntary contributions or a member tax set by leaders (0–10% of business income). All movements on the public-to-members ledger.
- **Roles:** Chair, Treasurer, CISO (security), General Counsel (legal), Analyst, Member. Permissions per role (spend limit, approve deals, launch cyber ops, hire lawyers).
- **Syndicates:** pool money for one deal too big for one member (buy a T5 business, a stadium, a takeover). Ownership is split pro rata via a contract (§8), so profits flow automatically.
- **Shared defense:** a Corporation can buy a **SOC (security operations center)** that adds a defense bonus to every member, scaling with treasury spend. Joining an alliance is how mid-size players survive whales.
- **Shared intel:** a research feed members can publish into (AI signals, tips, rumors about rivals). Leaking alliance intel to another alliance is the classic betrayal (espionage, Tape).
- **Joint cyber ops:** combine several members' cyber AIs against one target for a bigger attack score. Detection risk is shared, and the court can charge every participant (Tape).
- **Alliance HQ** in a city raises that district's desirability (Quant's real-estate model), so alliances cluster and fight over districts.

### 7.3 Alliance conflict

- **Rivalries:** declare a public rivalry with another alliance for a league week. Rival-vs-rival actions (shorting their IPOs, takeover bids, legal suits, hacks) score points on a rivalry board. Winner gets a cosmetic trophy for their HQ. Purely a framing layer on existing systems, cheap to build, great for drama.
- **Mergers and acquisitions:** two alliances can merge by vote. An alliance can buy a majority of another's member companies (player takeovers per Quant).
- **Betrayal:** a Treasurer can steal from the treasury. It's possible, and it's **embezzlement**: an automatic, high-detection court case, the betrayer's reputation is marked forever. Drama with consequences.

### 7.4 Leaving and kicking

- Leaving costs a cooldown (48 h before joining another alliance) so nobody hops alliances for perks or spies by joining and leaving.
- Kicked members keep their own assets; syndicate stakes stay as contract holdings.

## 8. Player-to-player trading and contracts

### 8.1 Markets between players

| Market | What trades | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Exchanges (Quant) | stocks, crypto, commodities, bonds, derivatives, player IPOs | per-tick batch auction |
| Business & property exchange | businesses, buildings, land | listings + bids, settles at tick, 2% tax, Quant's off-market price checks |
| Collectibles auction house | watches, art, cars (Velvet) | English auctions ending at a set tick, with anti-snipe extension (+12 ticks if a bid lands in the last 12) |
| Data market | data sets (Ledger) | listings; buyer gets a license, seller keeps the original |
| Model market | trained models, licensed not sold | rental per game day; licensed models are watermarked (Ledger/Tape) |
| Services | marketing, property management, law, security, intelligence (Quant's player-to-player businesses) | service contracts |

### 8.2 The contract engine

Contracts are the deepest multiplayer system: they let players build relationships the designers never scripted, while staying enforceable. No free-text legalese: a contract is a **typed bundle of clauses** that the engine can execute.

Clause library (v1 → full):

| Clause | Example | Enforcement |
|---|---|---|
| Transfer | "I pay $2M, you give me Café #3" | atomic swap at a tick |
| Loan | "$10M at 8%/game year, 1 game year, collateral: Tower B" | auto-repay schedule; default → collateral seized |
| Lease | "Rent my office for $40k/game week" | recurring transfer |
| Revenue share | "You get 20% of this business's profit for 2 game years" | auto payout each game day |
| Equity stake | syndicate splits | pro-rata payouts, voting weight |
| Option | "Right to buy my stake at $X before tick T, premium $Y" | exercise button |
| Service level | "Your security firm guarantees defense +15; refund if breached" | engine checks breach events |
| Non-compete | "Won't open a café in Glasshill for 1 game year" | engine blocks the action, or penalty clause |
| NDA | "Won't share data set D" | breach detection via provenance → court (Tape) |
| Bounty | "$5M to whoever proves player X committed fraud" | paid on a court conviction (legal bounty only; hiring someone to hack is a crime, Tape's call) |
| Penalty | "$1M if any clause above breaks" | automatic or via court |

- **Escrow:** money and assets in a contract are held by the **Clearing House** (a fictional neutral institution) until conditions are met. No "trust me" scams for the parts the engine can verify.
- **What the engine can't verify** (e.g. "I'll vote with you") becomes a court claim. The court (Tape) reads the contract and the event log.
- **Templates:** common contracts as one-tap templates ("Sell business", "Loan with collateral", "Join syndicate"), advanced builder for pros.
- **Fees:** 0.5% contract registration (sink) + Quant's 2% P2P transfer tax on value moved.
- **Reputation shown at signing:** counterparty's default history, convictions, seasons played.
- **Anti-funneling:** contracts with off-market terms (price > 10% from fair value, loans at 0%, revenue share to a new account) are flagged and capped by account age (§10).

### 8.3 Public filings (new idea, big one)

Like real-world disclosure rules: when a player crosses ownership thresholds or gets big, the world learns about it with a delay:
- Holding ≥ 5% of any company → public filing within 1 game day.
- Top 200 NWI players publish a **quarterly holdings report** (top 10 positions) at each game quarter end, 1 game week delayed.
- Insider trades on your own IPO'd company are public (and a legal trap if you lie, Tape).

This creates a whole metagame: tracking whales, front-running their disclosed holdings, hiding size through syndicates or AI execution algos, and suing people who don't file. Real finance does this; it's free content.

### 8.4 Social layer

- **Chat:** world channel (slow mode), league group, alliance (with ops channel), DMs (friends or contract counterparties only, to cut spam). Text only at launch; profanity filter, report button, mute/block. Moderation tooling in §11.
- **Feed:** a world "Wire" mixing news, public filings, big trades, court verdicts, alliance events, and follows.
- **Follow players:** see their public profile, filings, titles, and trade receipts they choose to share.
- **Shared receipts:** one tap to share a trade, a hack defended, or a verdict as an image card (marketing loop for Velvet's design).
- **Notifications:** push (Web Push for installed PWAs, native push in Capacitor), email digest. Categories: under attack, margin call, contract needs signature, lawsuit filed, league result, rival moved. Hard cap ~5 pushes per day unless under active attack.

## 10. Anti-cheat and integrity

### 10.1 Threats and counters

| Threat | Counter |
|---|---|
| Client tampering (fake balances, fake orders) | Server authority; client sends intents only; all validation server-side |
| Botting the UI with scripts (outside the in-game AI) | Rate limits per account (e.g. 10 intents/tick human max), input timing analysis, no advantage from speed (batching), and in-game AIs are cheaper than scripting anyway. Mild policy: automation outside the game's AI is against ToS, enforced on abuse patterns (24/7 activity, perfectly periodic inputs) |
| Multi-accounting / alt funneling | Device fingerprint, IP/ASN clustering, payment and email signals, behavior similarity, off-market trade detection (Quant), P2P caps by account age (day 1: $10k/day, day 7: $1M/day, day 21: uncapped below 10% of net worth), no P2P for guests |
| Collusion (two "rivals" farming lawsuits, fake hacks, wash trades) | Graph analysis on counterparties: pairs with repeated one-directional value flow get flagged; court settlements between linked accounts are voided |
| Real-money trading (selling accounts or in-game cash for real money) | ToS ban, P2P caps, flags on large transfers to new accounts, handle-change limits. No cash-out exists so the market stays small |
| Exploiting bugs | Event log lets us find every beneficiary of a bug; targeted rollback of exploit gains (not a world rollback) via compensating ledger entries |
| Account theft (real) | 2FA for top players, new-device alerts, 24 h hold on large transfers after a password/email change |
| Harassment and griefing | Chat moderation, block lists, protection period for new players, Tape's design caps on repeat targeting (same attacker vs same victim cooldown) |
| Timing attacks on news/seed | Batches close before publish, seed secret, commit-reveal entropy (§3.3) |
| Insider abuse (staff, us) | Admin actions are ledger events too, visible in an internal audit log; no admin can mint money without a reason code and second approval |

### 10.2 Enforcement ladder

Warning → asset clawback → season ban (character frozen, removed from boards) → account ban. All enforcement is logged with evidence; appeals via support form. Shadow pools (flagged players matched only with each other in leagues) for low-confidence cases.

### 10.3 Integrity telemetry

Dashboard per world (feeds Quant's stabilizer too): money minted vs destroyed per tick by reason code, top 20 wealth deltas per game day with explanation breakdown, P2P flow graph, contract defaults, flagged accounts, tick timing.

## 11. Live ops and admin

- **Admin console (internal web app):** search players, view ledgers and event history, freeze accounts, issue compensations (with second approval), inject or cancel news events, pause a world (ticks stop, clients show "Market halted"), adjust config knobs Quant exposes, schedule events, read moderation reports.
- **Market halt** is diegetic: "Trading halted by the Exchange" so outages and emergency fixes don't break immersion.
- **Deploys between ticks:** the world process supports graceful handover (new version loads latest snapshot + log, takes over at a tick boundary). Target zero missed ticks per deploy.
- **Feature flags** per world for staged rollouts (try a rule in a flash world, then 1 season world, then all).
- **Event scheduler:** central banks, earnings, finale, holiday events, all as data.

## 14. MVP vs full build

Build order is chosen so the game is **fun alone before it's multiplayer**, and multiplayer before it's adversarial. Each phase ends with Tape's PASS.

### Phase 0 — Single-player sandbox (proves the fun)
- Next.js app, engine runs **in the browser** (fine because there's nothing to cheat against yet), local save (IndexedDB) with export/import.
- Quant's MVP market slice: 1 city, 30 stocks, 5 crypto, gold/oil, 15 businesses, 3 districts.
- Screens: terminal, portfolio, businesses, city, news, simple progression.
- Tick speed slider (1×, 4×, 20×) for testing.
- Exit check: a new player understands the loop in 10 minutes and wants to come back.

### Phase 1 — Online single world (closed alpha, ~150 CCU)
- Accounts (guest + email + Google), world process, gateway, Postgres event log, snapshots.
- Same content as phase 0, now shared: shared prices, shared city demand, batch auction with real players.
- World + global leaderboard, basic profile, world chat, Wire feed, "While You Were Away".
- Admin console v1 (search, freeze, halt, compensate).
- Exit check: 4,000 CCU synthetic load test passes p99 tick < 2.5 s; 2 weeks of alpha with no ledger mismatch.

### Phase 2 — Seasons and social (open beta)
- Season lifecycle, settlement, Hall of Fame, leagues, multiple boards.
- Alliances (Partnership, Firm), syndicates, contract engine v1 (transfer, loan, lease, revenue share), escrow.
- Business/property exchange, auction house, public filings.
- PWA install + Web Push. French localization.
- Anti-cheat v1 (alt detection, P2P caps, collusion flags).

### Phase 3 — The adversarial layer (with Tape and Ledger)
- AI tech tree, trading AIs, cyber attack/defense, distillation, court system, insurance.
- Corporations and Conglomerates, joint ops, rivalries.
- Legacy world, flash worlds, Champions Cup.
- Derivatives and IPOs/takeovers from Quant's later layers.

### Phase 4 — Native apps and scale
- Capacitor iOS/Android, native push, haptics, biometric unlock, store releases.
- Multiple worlds per region, automated world provisioning, standby failover.
- Replays, season recap videos/cards, spectator pages.

**Rough effort:** with the team's current setup, Phase 0 is the realistic first build target and could be a few focused weeks. Phases 1–2 are where most engineering goes (server, persistence, contracts). I'd rather not promise dates until Phase 0 exists and we know our real velocity.
