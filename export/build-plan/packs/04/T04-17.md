## G4. Time (00 §5.1)

5 real seconds per tick; 720 ticks per game day (= 1 real hour); 30 ticks per game hour; one tick = 2 game minutes. Week = 7 game days (Mon–Fri trading, Sat–Sun weekend). Quarter = 13 weeks = 91 game days, months 28/28/35 days. Year = 364 game days = 262,080 ticks. Chapter = 8 real weeks = 1,344 game days. Real day = 24 game days.
Trading day tick-of-day `t`: pre-market 0–59; opening auction t=60; continuous 61–539; closing auction t=540; after-hours 541–719. Accruals (interest, rent, wages, fees) post at day close t=719. Business operations run once per game hour, staggered: an entity runs when `tickOfDay mod 30 == fnv1a32(id) mod 30`.

## G5. Tick pipeline (00 §5.2)

```
P0 ingest  P1 validate  P2 calendar  P3 world(16)  P4 npc(05,16)  P5 ai(11)  P6 match(05,06)
P7 clear(07)  P8 finance(07,08)  P9 real(09,10)  P10 conflict(12)  P11 law(13)  P12 social(14,15)
P13 ledger(03: commit postings atomically, settle/release holds, assert invariants)  P14 emit
```
A phase may read any committed state and the outputs of earlier phases of this tick; it writes only its own module's tables and queues postings for P13. Postings never commit before P13. A failed invariant rolls the tick back and replays it in safe mode. Consequences of tick N that other modules react to land in tick N+1 (via `ctx.send(..., 'NEXT')` or events).

### G6.1 Ports for later modules (dependency inversion)

A module may never import a module built after it. When its rules need data from a later module (for example 05 needs fundamental values from 09 or index weights from 08), it declares a **port**: a TypeScript interface in its own `ports.ts` (e.g. `MarketPorts`), and its factory takes the implementations: `createMarketsModule(ports: Partial<MarketPorts>)`. The later module exports an implementation of that port, and `packages/engine/index.ts` wires them when building the world. A missing port uses the default the spec gives for "module not present" (stated in the card), never an invented rule.

## G8. Determinism rules (02 §3.7, 19 Rule 3.6.2) — lint-enforced in packages/engine

Forbidden in engine code: `Date`, `performance`, `Math.random`, `crypto` randomness, timers, `process`, `globalThis`, `Intl`, `toLocaleString`, `localeCompare`, `async/await`/Promises, `for…in`, iterating `Object.keys/values/entries` of map-like objects, `Map`/`Set` iteration where order matters (use `SortedMap`/`SortedSet`), `Array.sort` without a total comparator (use `sortBy`), `JSON.stringify` for hashing/persistence (use `canonEncode`), `WeakMap/WeakRef`, `Math.exp/log/pow/sin/cos/tan/atan2/cbrt/hypot/...` (use `dmath`), `Math.round` (use `roundHalfEven`), `Number` for money or quantities. Allowed raw math: `+ - * /`, `Math.sqrt/abs/floor/ceil/trunc/min/max/sign/fround/imul/clz32`.
NaN/Infinity are illegal in state. Ties are broken by entity id then intent id. Iterate collections only in sorted order. Rejection sampling is forbidden; each variate uses a fixed number of draws. A module may draw only from its own streams. Split money into parts only with `allocate`. No exceptions for control flow: report impossible states with `ctx.fail`.

## Goal
Step 11 of the macro day step: decide when the world gets ripples (small one-target news moves), storms (multi-asset pushes) and majors (the crisis catalog of T04-18), under hard constraints that keep crises fair: one major at a time, three real weeks between major triggers, none in a chapter's last 48 game days or first 24, suppression after a player-caused major, triggers rotating across three UTC bands so no timezone always sleeps through them, crisis weeks announced 48 game days ahead. ## Implementation steps
1. **Fixed draw order (§3.19, G8).** At day close step 11, on `S.crisis()` (04's `macro:events` and `macro:events:kind` mapped to one stream, fixed order): (1) `u_r` → ripple count `poissonInverse(u_r, macro_ripple_rate)` (1/7 per game day); (2) 3 ripple slots × 5 draws (kind, target, sign, size, length) always drawn, a slot used only if `slot < count` and fewer than 3 ripples are active; (3) storm: start u, kind, precursor length; (4) major: start u, kind, severity, precursor length, contagion length. 24 draws every day, used or not. Chapter start adds 2 draws (crisis-week yes/no, week). Bloc choice for kinds that need it uses `chooseBlocs(store, kind, u_kind)` with the same kind draw (T04-18 documents the split of the uniform).
2. **Crisis week (§3.16.1).** At chapter start: with probability 0.6 a crisis week in real week `w = 2 + floor(u × 6)` of the chapter (168 game days each), `startTick = chapterStart + (w − 1) × 168 × 720`; announced 48 game days ahead: at `startTick − 48 × 720` write a `HookCall { owner: '16', hook: 'WIRE_ITEM', args: { key: 'crisis_week_announced', startTick, endTick } }` and set `crisisWeek.announcedTick` (the window then appears in T04-20's public calendar view). Inside it: storm rate × 2, a forced storm on its day 3 if none started yet in it, major rate × 1.5. At most one per chapter (`crisisWeekUsedThisChapter`).
3. **Ripples.** Kind uniform over RippleKind; target via `rippleEffect` (T04-18: sector weighted by sector size, commodity uniform, city weighted by activity), sign 50/50, size `U(0.03, 0.08)`, length `1 + floor(3u)` game days; no precursor; at most 3 active. They start at the next tick (TRIGGER phase immediately; public through 16 news).
4. **Storms.** If none active and ≥ 7 game days since the last ended and not in the first 24 game days of the chapter: start with probability `1 − e^(−rate)`, rate `macro_storm_rate` = 1/91 (× 2 in a crisis week); kind uniform over StormKind; precursor `1 + floor(2u)` game days; active until the precursor plus the kind's effect length (`STORM_EFFECT_DAYS`) has passed, then ENDED.
5. **Majors (§3.16.2, §3.16.3).** `λ_m = macro_major_base_rate × V(t)` (× 1.5 in a crisis week), `V = Σ_k elig_k × base_k × e^{vuln_k} / 3.0` from T04-18. Kind: cumulative weights in catalog order, pick with the kind draw. Severity `0.6 + 0.8u`; precursor `8 + 8u` game days (≥ 8); contagion `5 + 25u` game days; planned trigger tick = `plannedTrigger(header, precursorEnd, timezoneBandNext)` using 02's tick→UTC map (bands 00–08, 08–16, 16–24 UTC). Constraints checked against the planned trigger: no active major (PRECURSOR to ENDED), ≥ `macro_major_min_gap_days` (504) game days since the last major trigger, trigger not in the final 48 game days of the chapter, start not in the first 24 game days, `playerCausedSuppressUntil` passed. If allowed and `u < 1 − e^(−λ_m)`: `startEvent`, then rotate `timezoneBandNext` (0 → 1 → 2 → 0). Edge 9: a crisis that spans a chapter boundary continues; only new triggers obey the final-48 rule; chapter counters reset.
6. **WorldEvent record.** `startEvent` writes `mac.event` with hidden `precursorStartTick`, `triggerTick`, `plannedEndTick`, `severity`, the shock vectors (T04-18), `causeChain` (first entry: the scheduler key and the vulnerability terms that dominated). It emits `macro.event.internal` SEALED with the full plan. Nothing public before the trigger (exploit 3, 04#37).
7. **Phases (`stepPhases`).** PRECURSOR: precursor shocks active (real state changes players can observe: spreads, valuations, guidance, data), no announcement, `publicEventView.phase = 'UNANNOUNCED'` and the event is not listed by `activeWorldEvents`. At `triggerTick`: TRIGGER (1 game day), trigger shocks and hooks applied (T04-18 `applyEventShocks`), `publicTitleKey` set, `macro.event.trigger` PUBLIC (id, tier, kind, blocs, publicTitleKey; no planned length, no planned moves). Then CONTAGION for the planned contagion days; POLICY starts (overlapping CONTAGION) at the first tick a CB emergency meeting or a crisis-specific response for the event fires (`crisisResponseFired`); RECOVERY after CONTAGION ends, until `F < 40` and `FSI < 0.3` for 10 consecutive trading days or 180 game days; then ENDED with `macro.event.ended` PUBLIC (measured outcomes from `measureOutcomes`, for the archive). Each change emits `macro.event.phase` PUBLIC. Shocks decay on their own schedules regardless of phase labels, and the CONTAGION end is the only place the planned length shows, after it happens.
8. **Player-caused majors.** An emergent event with origin PLAYER_CAUSED (T04-18) sets `playerCausedSuppressUntil = tick + 336 × 720`. Emergent majors are never blocked and count for later constraints (they set the last trigger tick). Edge 8: an emergent label during an active major attaches as a sub-phase (a `causeChain` entry and a `macro.event.phase` with the sub-label) rather than a second major.
9. Ownership note (R08): 16 §3.7 lists its own shock rates for non-financial world shocks; those are 16's content drawn on 16's streams and never start a 04 tier event. Only this scheduler starts RIPPLE, STORM and MAJOR events.

# SPEC EXCERPTS (verbatim; binding game rules)

**[04 §1 Purpose]** (context)

## §1 Purpose

The macro layer is the one shared state every price, business, loan and property in the world reads (V3 Part 2.4). It exists so that:

1. **Prices have reasons.** A rate hike, a CPI surprise, a downgrade or a currency defence hits bonds, mortgages, growth stocks, bank margins, cap rates, carry trades and leveraged funds on the same tick, through named transmission channels that the "Why it moved" ladder (05 §3.12) can cite.
2. **Recessions are caused, not random** (old 01 §5.3). Imbalances (credit growth, valuation stretch, an inverted curve, tight real rates, oil shocks) raise the hazard of a downturn, so a careful player can see one building.
3. **Central banks, treasuries and sovereign bond markets are real machinery** (V3 Part 3.2): policy rates with a corridor, reserves, standing facilities, asset purchases, forward guidance, deficits that are financed by auctions, debt that can be downgraded and restructured, currencies that can be defended and can break.
4. **Crises emerge from rules** (V3 T2) and also arrive on a calibrated schedule so the world has rhythm (one major crisis in about 35% of chapters, storms every game quarter, ripples every game week).
5. **The money stays honest.** The Stabilizer controller keeps the player economy on its designed path, and the Money Supply Index deflates rankings so inflation cannot pump rank (old 01 §8.2–8.3).

#### §3.16.2 Constraints on scheduled majors
1. At most one major active (from PRECURSOR to ENDED).
2. At least `PARAM.macro_major_min_gap_days = 504` game days (3 real weeks) between the trigger ticks of two majors.
3. No scheduled major may trigger in the final 48 game days of a chapter (no ladder lottery; 15 also averages the finish).
4. After a PLAYER_CAUSED major, scheduled majors are suppressed for 336 game days (2 real weeks).
5. Emergent majors (§3.16.5) are never blocked; they count toward 1–4 for later scheduling.
6. No scheduled MAJOR or STORM may start in the first 24 game days (24 real hours) of a chapter (aligned with 16 §3.7.3; ruling in 24). 04 §3.16 is authoritative for crisis tiers, rates, constraints and the Crisis Week; 16 §3.7 draws the non-financial shock content within these limits.

#### §3.16.3 Scheduling algorithm (macro day step, step 11)
1. Ripples: draw count `~ Poisson(1/7)` on `macro:events`; for each, pick a kind uniformly from RippleKind, then a target (sector weighted by sector size, commodity uniformly, city weighted by activity), sign 50/50, size `U(0.03, 0.08)`, length `U(1, 3)` game days. Within COMMODITY_SUPPLY and SECTOR_SHOCK (and storm CREDIT_WOBBLE), a sub-def is drawn by weight (E-35 §5, PROVISIONAL [Quant]; T04-18 lists them: drought, crop disease, chip fab, shipping lane with C18 delays, company-scope recall/scandal/cyber, data-center with C17, BANK_RUN_LOCAL); sub-defs never change the kind rates, so the total stays 0.154 per GD.
2. Storms: if none active and ≥ 7 days since the last ended: start with probability `1 − e^(−rate)`, rate 1/91 (× 2 in a crisis week; plus a forced storm on day 3 of a crisis week if none happened); kind uniformly from StormKind; precursor 1–2 game days.
3. Majors: if constraints allow, start with probability `1 − e^(−λ_m)`. Choose the kind with weights `elig_k × base_k × e^(vuln_k)` (second draw on `macro:events:kind`); bloc(s) as the table says; severity `U(0.6, 1.4)`; precursor length `U(8, 16)` game days; trigger tick = the first tick after the precursor whose real UTC hour (02's tick→UTC map) lies in the band `timezoneBandNext` (bands 00–08, 08–16, 16–24 UTC), then rotate the band.
4. During PRECURSOR the event applies its precursor shocks (real state changes that players can observe: spreads, valuations, guidance, data); it is not announced. At trigger, the trigger shocks apply and 16 publishes the first headline (`publicTitleKey` set).

## §8 Edge cases

1. **Policy at the ELB and the rule wants more easing:** rate stays at `elb`; QE logic (§3.8.5) takes over; guidance score is clamped at −1.
2. **Negative rates:** only EC and NA; the deposit facility rate can go negative, reversing the `CBK_DEPOSIT_INTEREST` direction (a charge, class SINK for player banks). 07 passes negative rates to deposits only for corporate deposits above 10M (DEFAULT).
3. **Hyperinflation guard:** `π_core` clamps at 60%; at > 25% for 91 days the hard-peg option opens (§3.13.5); CPI levels are floats (no overflow).
4. **Deflation:** `π_core` floors at −5%; real rates rise mechanically; the rule cuts toward ELB.
5. **A release day with a market-wide halt:** the release still publishes; 05 queues orders; the reopening auction absorbs it.
6. **Weekends:** macro day steps still run (state evolves); releases, meetings and auctions are on trading days only; FX regime breaks happen at the next `t = 45` of a trading day, except SC capital controls, which can start on a weekend day close if a weekend crisis trigger fires (crypto-driven contagion).
7. **Meeting on a day with an emergency already held that week:** the scheduled meeting still occurs; the emergency count resets each quarter.
8. **Two triggers on the same day close:** process in the fixed order of §3.1; only one major can be active; an emergent label during an active major is attached to it as a sub-phase rather than a second major.
9. **Chapter boundary inside a crisis:** crises continue; the chapter counters reset; the scheduler's "final 48 game days" rule applies only to new triggers.
10. **Auction with no player bids:** NPC ladder alone clears it; results are still published.
11. **Auction award rounding:** pro-rata remainders in lots go in hash order; the sum of awards never exceeds offered.
12. **A bidder's cash hold fails at settlement** (cannot happen because the hold is taken at submission; if a hold is released by 13 for a Held character, the bid is cancelled at P1 of the auction tick and the auction clears without it).
13. **Capital controls while a player holds SRL cash and an FDL margin call (07):** margin calls in another currency are met from that currency first; if impossible, 07 liquidates FDL positions; SRL cannot be converted at level ≥ 2. This is intended crisis risk and shown on the margin forecast (RSK-03).
14. **Restructuring vote by a holder whose bonds are lent out (07 securities lending):** the vote belongs to the borrower holding the bonds at the snapshot (as in real markets); lenders can recall before the snapshot.
15. **Bonds pledged as CB repo collateral when the line is restructured:** the CB revalues the new bonds; if collateral is short, 07 calls for top-up within 1 game day.
16. **Default of a bloc whose bonds are CCP collateral:** 07 haircut tables react through grades; no special rule.
17. **N_a collapses (world empties):** scale shrinks at most 0.5% per game day and floors at 0.2; nominal quantities shrink smoothly; outstanding debts are not rescaled (only flows and pools scale), so debt/GDP rises if the world shrinks fast, which is reported but capped in hazard z1 by the clamp.
18. **No 07 yet (early builds and unit tests):** the §3.14.4 fallback provides credit inputs; facility intents return `CBK_NOT_ELIGIBLE`.
19. **Release measurement noise moves a print across a threshold** (e.g. the Cycle Committee condition): the committee reads published prints, so noise can delay or hasten a declaration; this is intended.
20. **YCC and QE caps both binding:** the YCC bid shrinks to the remaining capacity; the cap can break; Wire item "YCC under pressure".
21. **Peg break while players hold FX forwards (06):** forwards settle at market; no special rule.
22. **Two blocs meet within the same tick** (cannot happen by calendar; meetings are on distinct trading days of the quarter).
23. **Missing input from another module this day:** last value holds; the event log records `macro.input.stale` (internal) for 20.
24. **Stabilizer with a tiny population:** OBSERVE mode below 03's `PARAM.stab_min_sample` engaged characters (03 §3.7.5); MSI per 03 §3.8.
25. **Stabilizer lever at a bound for more than 14 real days:** emits an internal alert to 21's admin console and 20; no automatic widening (Mathis or live-ops decide).

### §3.7 World events and shocks (layer 6)

**§3.7.1 Frequencies** (ROADMAP §2.8, made exact). Shocks are drawn at day close for the next game day from `rng('world.shock', day)`:
```
RIPPLE : Poisson rate PARAM.macro_ripple_rate = 1/7 per game day world-wide   (≈ 3.4 per real day; local, small)
STORM  : PARAM.macro_storm_rate = 1/91 per game day                            (≈ 1 per 3.8 real days; bloc or sector)
MAJOR  : drawn by 04 only (`macro_major_base_rate`, 04 §3.16); `major_exo_rate` retired (E-29)
```
Target: the probability that a chapter contains at least one MAJOR crisis of any cause is about 35% (`λ_total = −ln(0.65) ≈ 0.43` per chapter); the remaining `0.23` comes from endogenous crises (bank runs from 07, sovereign stress from 04, crashes from 05, contagion), which 16 does not draw but does count. The harness (20) tests the observed rate across 200 simulated chapters to lie in [25%, 45%]. The split between exogenous and endogenous MAJORs is 04's (`macro_major_base_rate`, constraint 5); 16 draws none (E-29).

**§3.7.2 Drawing.** Category by weights per tier (22 per-tier table; launch: WEATHER 0.25, GEOPOLITICAL 0.15, LABOR 0.12, HEALTH 0.05, TECH 0.10, ACCIDENT 0.12, DISCOVERY 0.06, COMMODITY_SUPPLY 0.10, CYBER_WAVE 0.05), × the chapter theme multiplier (15 §3.3, e.g. "Supply Squeeze" ×2 for COMMODITY_SUPPLY), × band rotation: the bloc or sector hit by the previous shock of the same tier gets weight × 0.5 for the next 2 draws. Then `ShockDef` by `baseWeight` within (tier, category), scope key uniformly among eligible (cities with population ≥ the def's minimum; commodities listed in 06). Magnitudes uniform in each effect's range.

**§3.7.3 Precursors and constraints.**
- Precursor (telegraph) window: MAJOR ≥ `PARAM.major_precursor_min_game_days = 8` (8–20), STORM 1–2 game days, RIPPLE none. During the window, 1–3 telegraph news items appear (WEATHER: forecasts with probability bands; GEOPOLITICAL: escalating statements; LABOR: strike ballot). A telegraphed event happens with probability 0.85 (15% de-escalate, recorded): hedging has value but is never certain.
- At most 1 MAJOR active at a time; MAJOR onsets at least `PARAM.macro_major_min_gap_days = 504` apart (21 real days).
- No MAJOR or STORM onset in the final 48 real hours of a chapter (the finish window, 15 §3.3), and none during the first 24 real hours of a chapter.
- After a player-caused major crisis (a cascade that 03 attributes to player entities, e.g. a player bank failure triggering contagion), exogenous MAJORs are suppressed for `PARAM.major_suppression_game_days = 336` (14 real days).
- **Crisis Week**: defined and drawn by 04 §3.16.1 (owner of crisis tiers and rates; ruling in 24): at most 1 per chapter, drawn at chapter start with probability 0.6 and placed in real weeks 2–7, announced 48 real hours ahead as a world event (theme from 22), storms ×2 with one guaranteed storm and the MAJOR rate ×1.5; no MAJOR is ever guaranteed. LiveOps (21) supplies only its theme copy and cannot schedule or force one.

**§3.7.4 Effects.** A shock's effects are routed to owner hooks only, each with magnitude, lag and duration (e.g. `06.supplyShock(commodity, −12%, lag 0, dur 30)`, `09.capacityShock(sector, city, −20%)`, `10.cityDamage(district, severity)`, `04.demandShock(bloc, −0.5% GDP)`, `07.creditTightening(bloc, +0.2)`, `08.catLoss(region, peril, severity)`, `05.volRegime(bloc, +1)`). 16 schedules them through the consequence scheduler (§3.8) so follow-on effects (insurance claims, reconstruction demand, price normalization) are recorded and explainable.

**§3.7.5 Labor and strikes.** Each UNION_LEADER actor covers 1–3 sectors in a bloc. At each strategic epoch: `p_strike = σ(−4 + 3·(realWageGap) + 2·(profitGrowth) − 1.5·u + 1.0·aggression)`, where `realWageGap = max(0, π − wageGrowth)/0.02`. A strike is telegraphed by a ballot (3–7 game days), lasts U(3, 21) game days, cuts that sector's capacity in the covered cities by 30–60% (09), and ends with a settlement raising wages by `0.5–1.5 × π` (09 wage costs). Player businesses in the sector can settle early with their own workers (09 wage intent) to keep operating at a cost.

**§3.7.6 Shock catalog** (22 holds about 120 defs; minimum at launch): RIPPLE: local storm, port delay, product recall, factory fire, celebrity scandal, small cyber incident at an NPC firm, local power cut, road closure. STORM: regional drought, hurricane on one coastal city, chip-fab outage, shipping-lane disruption, regional strike, data-center outage, crop disease, a mid-size bank run. MAJOR: pandemic wave, trade war between two blocs, major quake, global energy shock, systemic cyber wave (abstract: many firms offline), sovereign default scare, AI breakthrough shock (from §3.10), commodity super-squeeze. Cyber shocks are described only as outcomes (firms offline, data exposed); no techniques.
