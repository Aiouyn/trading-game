## G4. Time (00 §5.1)

5 real seconds per tick; 720 ticks per game day (= 1 real hour); 30 ticks per game hour; one tick = 2 game minutes. Week = 7 game days (Mon–Fri trading, Sat–Sun weekend). Quarter = 13 weeks = 91 game days, months 28/28/35 days. Year = 364 game days = 262,080 ticks. Chapter = 8 real weeks = 1,344 game days. Real day = 24 game days.
Trading day tick-of-day `t`: pre-market 0–59; opening auction t=60; continuous 61–539; closing auction t=540; after-hours 541–719. Accruals (interest, rent, wages, fees) post at day close t=719. Business operations run once per game hour, staggered: an entity runs when `tickOfDay mod 30 == fnv1a32(id) mod 30`.

## G5. Tick pipeline (00 §5.2)

```
P0 ingest  P1 validate  P2 calendar  P3 world(16)  P4 npc(05,16)  P5 ai(11)  P6 match(05,06)
P7 clear(07)  P8 finance(07,08)  P9 real(09,10)  P10 conflict(12)  P11 law(13)  P12 social(14,15)
P13 ledger(03: commit postings atomically, settle/release holds, assert invariants)  P14 emit
```
A phase may read any committed state and the outputs of earlier phases of this tick; it writes only its own module's tables and queues postings for P13. Postings never commit before P13. A failed invariant rolls the tick back and replays it in safe mode. Consequences of tick N that other modules react to land in tick N+1 (via `ctx.send(..., 'NEXT')` or events).

### G6.1 Ports for later modules (dependency inversion)

A module may never import a module built after it. When its rules need data from a later module (for example 05 needs fundamental values from 09 or index weights from 08), it declares a **port**: a TypeScript interface in its own `ports.ts` (e.g. `MarketPorts`), and its factory takes the implementations: `createMarketsModule(ports: Partial<MarketPorts>)`. The later module exports an implementation of that port, and `packages/engine/index.ts` wires them when building the world. A missing port uses the default the spec gives for "module not present" (stated in the card), never an invented rule.

## G8. Determinism rules (02 §3.7, 19 Rule 3.6.2) — lint-enforced in packages/engine

Forbidden in engine code: `Date`, `performance`, `Math.random`, `crypto` randomness, timers, `process`, `globalThis`, `Intl`, `toLocaleString`, `localeCompare`, `async/await`/Promises, `for…in`, iterating `Object.keys/values/entries` of map-like objects, `Map`/`Set` iteration where order matters (use `SortedMap`/`SortedSet`), `Array.sort` without a total comparator (use `sortBy`), `JSON.stringify` for hashing/persistence (use `canonEncode`), `WeakMap/WeakRef`, `Math.exp/log/pow/sin/cos/tan/atan2/cbrt/hypot/...` (use `dmath`), `Math.round` (use `roundHalfEven`), `Number` for money or quantities. Allowed raw math: `+ - * /`, `Math.sqrt/abs/floor/ceil/trunc/min/max/sign/fround/imul/clz32`.
NaN/Infinity are illegal in state. Ties are broken by entity id then intent id. Iterate collections only in sorted order. Rejection sampling is forbidden; each variate uses a fixed number of draws. A module may draw only from its own streams. Split money into parts only with `allocate`. No exceptions for control flow: report impossible states with `ctx.fail`.

**[12 §1 Purpose]** (context)

## §1 Purpose

1. Make wealth attackable and defendable so that the richest players have an edge but never immunity (L2 floor 1%).
2. Make crime a priced business: every op shows its expected cost, success band, detection band and the sanction schedule it maps to (13 §3.9). Target: a competent criminal's chapter outcome is 0.8–1.2× a competent honest player's, at 2–4× the variance (20 balance bands).
3. Implement Mathis's hidden-session model (L3): quiet intrusions plant concealed entries that accrue progress toward a 0.85 ceiling until noticed and disconnected; offline slows accrual; defense AI auto-scans by tier.
4. Provide the intelligence layer: scouting, dossiers, informants, counter-intelligence, so information is a contested resource.
5. Provide market-conduct conflict (squeezes, false stories, short campaigns, rebuttals) as first-class adversarial play that the regulator (13) polices.
6. Emit every fact as an event so that evidence is discoverable later (13) and nothing is ever perfectly clean.

Design principles kept from the old vision 03 §0: crime has EV; every op leaves evidence; rich have an edge not immunity; victims have recourse; no permanent ruin; punch-up cheap, punch-down expensive; legibility before commitment; every flow tagged; abstract fiction only.

### §3.11 Market-conduct conflict

Market ops are first-class adversarial play policed by the ECA regulator (13) through a surveillance score the markets engine computes (05 owns `surveillanceScore(playerId, window) ∈ [0,100]`; 12 and 13 consume it). 12 defines the player verbs; it never fines.

- **M1 Short campaign.** Build a short position across ticks, then publish findings on the Wire (15). If the findings are **true** (verifiable against engine state), it is lawful research and the price reacts via the belief model (05 §6.3, 16): a story's effect depends on reach (prestige, §7.7 of 14), source credibility, evidence quality and the target's response, never a fixed drop. Skeptics can trade against it. If the findings are **false**, it is manipulation (S5) and feeds the surveillance score. Covering into a decline the campaigner engineered with false claims is disgorged on conviction (13).
- **M2 Squeeze.** Accumulate a corner in a thin name or a recalled short-borrow and force covering. The markets engine's position caps (05: 25% float NPC, 49% player, short-interest cap 50%) bound it; crossing into coordinated cornering raises the surveillance score and can draw a CMA antitrust/conduct case (13).
- **M3 Pump & promote.** Promote a holding through a player news outlet (05/09) while holding it, then sell. Buy-promote-sell overlap is exactly what the surveillance score keys on; S5 manipulation.
- **M4 Rebuttal (defensive).** A targeted company publishes a verifiable rebuttal, improves disclosure (15 filings), or files a case (13). Defenses cost resources and do not guarantee recovery.

Market ops leave surveillance evidence rather than cyber forensics evidence, so they are caught by the regulator path (13 §regulators), not by scans.
