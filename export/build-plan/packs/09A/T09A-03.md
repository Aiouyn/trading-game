## G4. Time (00 §5.1)

5 real seconds per tick; 720 ticks per game day (= 1 real hour); 30 ticks per game hour; one tick = 2 game minutes. Week = 7 game days (Mon–Fri trading, Sat–Sun weekend). Quarter = 13 weeks = 91 game days, months 28/28/35 days. Year = 364 game days = 262,080 ticks. Chapter = 8 real weeks = 1,344 game days. Real day = 24 game days.
Trading day tick-of-day `t`: pre-market 0–59; opening auction t=60; continuous 61–539; closing auction t=540; after-hours 541–719. Accruals (interest, rent, wages, fees) post at day close t=719. Business operations run once per game hour, staggered: an entity runs when `tickOfDay mod 30 == fnv1a32(id) mod 30`.

## G5. Tick pipeline (00 §5.2)

```
P0 ingest  P1 validate  P2 calendar  P3 world(16)  P4 npc(05,16)  P5 ai(11)  P6 match(05,06)
P7 clear(07)  P8 finance(07,08)  P9 real(09,10)  P10 conflict(12)  P11 law(13)  P12 social(14,15)
P13 ledger(03: commit postings atomically, settle/release holds, assert invariants)  P14 emit
```
A phase may read any committed state and the outputs of earlier phases of this tick; it writes only its own module's tables and queues postings for P13. Postings never commit before P13. A failed invariant rolls the tick back and replays it in safe mode. Consequences of tick N that other modules react to land in tick N+1 (via `ctx.send(..., 'NEXT')` or events).

### G6.1 Ports for later modules (dependency inversion)

A module may never import a module built after it. When its rules need data from a later module (for example 05 needs fundamental values from 09 or index weights from 08), it declares a **port**: a TypeScript interface in its own `ports.ts` (e.g. `MarketPorts`), and its factory takes the implementations: `createMarketsModule(ports: Partial<MarketPorts>)`. The later module exports an implementation of that port, and `packages/engine/index.ts` wires them when building the world. A missing port uses the default the spec gives for "module not present" (stated in the card), never an invented rule.

## G8. Determinism rules (02 §3.7, 19 Rule 3.6.2) — lint-enforced in packages/engine

Forbidden in engine code: `Date`, `performance`, `Math.random`, `crypto` randomness, timers, `process`, `globalThis`, `Intl`, `toLocaleString`, `localeCompare`, `async/await`/Promises, `for…in`, iterating `Object.keys/values/entries` of map-like objects, `Map`/`Set` iteration where order matters (use `SortedMap`/`SortedSet`), `Array.sort` without a total comparator (use `sortBy`), `JSON.stringify` for hashing/persistence (use `canonEncode`), `WeakMap/WeakRef`, `Math.exp/log/pow/sin/cos/tan/atan2/cbrt/hypot/...` (use `dmath`), `Math.round` (use `roundHalfEven`), `Number` for money or quantities. Allowed raw math: `+ - * /`, `Math.sqrt/abs/floor/ceil/trunc/min/max/sign/fround/imul/clz32`.
NaN/Infinity are illegal in state. Ties are broken by entity id then intent id. Iterate collections only in sorted order. Rejection sampling is forbidden; each variate uses a fixed number of draws. A module may draw only from its own streams. Split money into parts only with `allocate`. No exceptions for control flow: report impossible states with `ctx.fail`.

## Goal
Compute how many units customers want in every market each game day and each game hour. Demand comes from the market's source (households, derived business activity, tourism, player services, none for commodity and flow-take types), scales with city population and income, the 04 output gap, seasonality, causal shocks and the Stabilizer demand lever, is split across districts by footfall, shaped by the hour profile and weekend factor, and responds at category level to the share-weighted average price so collusive price rises lose volume.

## Implementation steps
1. `householdDemand` exactly per 09 §3.2.2, then × `segShare` (the segment's share of the city category demand from 22; `perCapita` was calibrated per segment share in T09A-02). `income(c)`, `pop(c)` from 10 `cityStats` (10/16 grow them with `N_a`); `incomeRef` = the reference city's genesis income (stored in `biz.calib` meta row `id:'__ref'` by this task's first run if absent); `outputGap` from 04 for the city's bloc; `season = ref.seasonality[weekOfYear]`; `stab = leverValue(read,'L1_demand')`.
2. `shock(c, cat)` is the product of: `ports.world.demandShock(cityId, category, districtId|null, tick)` (16: festival 1.4 for 1 game day, heatwave, embargo, strikes in the city; each from a causal event, W05) and the closure factor: a market whose district (DISTRICT scope) or city (CITY scope) is under an active `ports.insurance.closures` closure gets 0 for those days (catastrophe closure; demand returns when the closure ends).
3. BUSINESS_DERIVED: `activity` = `Market.soldYesterday` summed over the driver category's markets in the city (player and NPC field units, recorded by the hourly loop), or a 10/11 quantity named in 22 (`driverQty`: `'DC_MW'` → `ports.ai.dcMegawatts(cityId)`, `'PROP_TX'` → `propertyTransactions`, `'PROPERTIES'` → `ports.social.propertyCountIndex(cityId)` × reference count, `'PORT_THROUGHPUT'` → derived from the port terminal market's units). GOVERNMENT categories: `demandDay = 0` (procured only through tenders, T09A-14 uses the category's government volume). COMMODITY_MARKET and FLOW_TAKE: `demandDay = 0` (no pool; archetype formulas in T09A-05/06).
4. District split for DISTRICT-scope markets: `D_district = D_city × footfall(d, cat) / Σ footfall` (10's weights). Edge 3: a market with zero participants still gets its demand (the explorer and entry estimates use it).
5. `computeDailyDemand` (P9, `cal.tickOfDay === 0`): iterate `biz.market` in id order; compute `demandDay`; set `soldYesterday = soldToday`, `soldToday = 0`, `npcSoldToday = 0`, `revenueToday = 0`; put the row. Order matters: derived demand reads `soldYesterday` of driver markets, so first roll every market's `soldYesterday` (pass 1), then compute demand (pass 2).
6. `hourlyDemand = dDay × hourProfile[h] × (weekend ? weekendFactor : 1)` for `h ∈ hoursOpen`, else 0; weekend = `cal.weekday ∈ {5, 6}` (Sat, Sun; G4 week Mon to Fri trading).
7. `priceResponse` (09 §3.3.5): the market's effective demand for the hour is `D_hour × (avgPrice / refPrice)^(−η)` with `avgPrice` the share-weighted average including the NPC field at ref price (`shareWeightedAvgPrice`), η = 22 `priceElasticity`. Computed with `dmath.pow`. The hourly loop (T09A-09) calls it with the shares of the previous recompute.

# SPEC EXCERPTS (verbatim; binding game rules)

**[09 §1 Purpose]** (context)

## §1 Purpose

Businesses are the steady engine of wealth (the tycoon half; markets are the volatile half, 01 §4). Companies are how a player turns businesses into an institution: found a company, build products, hire, borrow, compete for customers, publish earnings, list on the exchange, acquire rivals, survive distress.

What the module guarantees:
1. **Real shared competition.** Every business sells into a demand pool shared with every other player and NPC competitor in its market (city or district × product category × segment). A logit share model decides who wins customers (01 §4.1). Similar firms coexist; nothing consumes a unique worldwide slot except named unique infrastructure (C03).
2. **Distinct economics.** Each business belongs to one of 16 economic archetypes with its own formula (footfall retail, local service, B2B contract, commodity producer, processor, platform, subscription, hit-driven, tourism, transport capacity, regulated utility, compute provider, trial binary, financial institution, trophy, flip trading). A niche is a combination of archetype, category, segment, location, operating model and promise (C02), not just a new name (C04, R01).
3. **Customers with requirements.** NPC customers post tenders with price caps, reliability, certification, response time and confidentiality requirements; switching takes effort; concentration has risks (C05–C08).
4. **Company building in depth** (Mathis directive 2026-10-09, STONKS-9800 spirit): R&D, product lines, marketing, hiring, production and capacity, pricing, expansion, financing; real operating economics; filings and earnings that feed the market; IPO onto the same exchange.
5. **Corporate finance and control**: capital structure, covenants, dividends, buybacks, issuance, IPOs, takeovers and defences, activism, LBOs, restructuring with absolute priority. A bankrupt company is a market, not just a delisting (V3 3.7).
6. **Honest money.** Revenue from NPC customers is a faucet, costs paid to NPCs are sinks, everything between players is a transfer; diminishing returns (logit share, exponential upgrade costs, finite demand) and the offline cap keep business income from running away (01 §8.3).

#### §3.2.2 Daily demand (computed at t = 0 each game day, P9)

```
HOUSEHOLD categories:
  D_city = perCapita(cat) × pop(c) × (income(c) / incomeRef)^ε(cat) × (1 + cycleSens(cat) × outputGap(bloc)) × season(cat, week) × shock(c, cat) × stab
BUSINESS_DERIVED categories (input-output, C10):
  D_city = Σ_driver ioCoef(cat, driverCat) × activity(c, driverCat) × stab
     activity = units sold yesterday by all businesses (player + NPC field) of the driver category in c, or a 10/11 quantity
     (e.g. cooling contractor ← data-center MW; moving company ← property transactions; cleaning ← properties owned by players and NPC)
TOURISM: D_city = tourismIndex(c) (10/16) × roomNightsPerIndex(cat) × season × stab
GOVERNMENT: procured only through tenders (§3.9)
PLAYER_SERVICES: demand is the sum of player purchases through service contracts (§3.9.6) plus an NPC share = perCapita × pop × stab
COMMODITY_MARKET: no demand pool; output sells at the 06 spot price (§3.4.2)
FLOW_TAKE: no demand pool; revenue = take rate × an observed flow (§3.4.2)
District split for DISTRICT-scope markets: D_district = D_city × footfall(district, cat) / Σ footfall   (10 supplies footfall weights)
```

`stab` is the Stabilizer demand lever (03/04 own its value; range [0.90, 1.10], 01 §8.2 lever 1). `pop` and `income` come from 10/16 and grow with the city (which grows with `N_a`, 01 §14.1), so demand scales with activity-weighted players, never sign-ups. `shock` aggregates active events (festival +40% for 1 game day, heatwave, catastrophe closures from 08, strikes in the city, embargoes), each from a causal event (W05).

**[09 §3.3.5]** (from spec file 09-BUSINESSES-AND-COMPANIES.md)

#### §3.3.5 Category price response

The whole market's demand responds to the share-weighted average price: `D_eff = D × (avgPrice / refPrice)^(−η)` with η = `priceElasticity` (22, typical 0.3–1.2). This blocks collusion where all players in a market raise prices together (the NPC field also stays at ref price).
