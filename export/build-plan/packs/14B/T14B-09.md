## G4. Time (00 §5.1)

5 real seconds per tick; 720 ticks per game day (= 1 real hour); 30 ticks per game hour; one tick = 2 game minutes. Week = 7 game days (Mon–Fri trading, Sat–Sun weekend). Quarter = 13 weeks = 91 game days, months 28/28/35 days. Year = 364 game days = 262,080 ticks. Chapter = 8 real weeks = 1,344 game days. Real day = 24 game days.
Trading day tick-of-day `t`: pre-market 0–59; opening auction t=60; continuous 61–539; closing auction t=540; after-hours 541–719. Accruals (interest, rent, wages, fees) post at day close t=719. Business operations run once per game hour, staggered: an entity runs when `tickOfDay mod 30 == fnv1a32(id) mod 30`.

## G5. Tick pipeline (00 §5.2)

```
P0 ingest  P1 validate  P2 calendar  P3 world(16)  P4 npc(05,16)  P5 ai(11)  P6 match(05,06)
P7 clear(07)  P8 finance(07,08)  P9 real(09,10)  P10 conflict(12)  P11 law(13)  P12 social(14,15)
P13 ledger(03: commit postings atomically, settle/release holds, assert invariants)  P14 emit
```
A phase may read any committed state and the outputs of earlier phases of this tick; it writes only its own module's tables and queues postings for P13. Postings never commit before P13. A failed invariant rolls the tick back and replays it in safe mode. Consequences of tick N that other modules react to land in tick N+1 (via `ctx.send(..., 'NEXT')` or events).

### G6.1 Ports for later modules (dependency inversion)

A module may never import a module built after it. When its rules need data from a later module (for example 05 needs fundamental values from 09 or index weights from 08), it declares a **port**: a TypeScript interface in its own `ports.ts` (e.g. `MarketPorts`), and its factory takes the implementations: `createMarketsModule(ports: Partial<MarketPorts>)`. The later module exports an implementation of that port, and `packages/engine/index.ts` wires them when building the world. A missing port uses the default the spec gives for "module not present" (stated in the card), never an invented rule.

## G8. Determinism rules (02 §3.7, 19 Rule 3.6.2) — lint-enforced in packages/engine

Forbidden in engine code: `Date`, `performance`, `Math.random`, `crypto` randomness, timers, `process`, `globalThis`, `Intl`, `toLocaleString`, `localeCompare`, `async/await`/Promises, `for…in`, iterating `Object.keys/values/entries` of map-like objects, `Map`/`Set` iteration where order matters (use `SortedMap`/`SortedSet`), `Array.sort` without a total comparator (use `sortBy`), `JSON.stringify` for hashing/persistence (use `canonEncode`), `WeakMap/WeakRef`, `Math.exp/log/pow/sin/cos/tan/atan2/cbrt/hypot/...` (use `dmath`), `Math.round` (use `roundHalfEven`), `Number` for money or quantities. Allowed raw math: `+ - * /`, `Math.sqrt/abs/floor/ceil/trunc/min/max/sign/fround/imul/clz32`.
NaN/Infinity are illegal in state. Ties are broken by entity id then intent id. Iterate collections only in sorted order. Rejection sampling is forbidden; each variate uses a fixed number of draws. A module may draw only from its own streams. Split money into parts only with `allocate`. No exceptions for control flow: report impossible states with `ctx.fail`.

# SPEC EXCERPTS (verbatim; binding game rules)

**[14 §1 Purpose]** (context)

## §1 Purpose

1. **Make money feel like something.** Rank, home, shell, possessions and titles turn net worth into a place and an identity. A player should feel the jump from $50k to $5M without reading a number (old 04 §0.1).
2. **Drain money in a way players want.** Lifestyle is the economy's most voluntary sink. Mass luxury loses value on purchase and fades in Prestige, so it must be refreshed; collectibles hold value but carry upkeep (old 04 §5.1).
3. **Give small, real, capped advantages** for spending (access, convenience, talent, collateral, reach, physical security) so spending is a decision, never a cash faucet (old 04 §0.4).
4. **Keep three separate social axes**: Prestige (status from what you own and display), Reputation (trust from clean conduct), Notoriety (fame from crime). A player can be high on all three (old 04 §7.1, bible decision 5).
5. **Remember.** The world never resets (L1), so Legacy, Houses and succession give a permanent public history: founding plaques, generations, heirlooms, chapter portraits (V3 Part 9).
6. **Respect the player.** No demotion, no punishing streaks, no real-money path to any of this (L7), quiet money stays a valid strategy (old 04 §7.8).

During PROBATE: no outbound P2P transfers or gifts, no new contracts as payer, no Fresh Start, no handle change. Trading, businesses and defense continue.

| $1B – $10B | 20% |
| > $10B | 30% |

### §3.12 Repo

#### §3.12.1 Venues and participants
| Venue | Collateral | Who | Clearing |
|---|---|---|---|
| CCP_GC | Government bond baskets per bloc (any bond of that government) | Banks, LIC4 players, funds (08), MMFs | TCH REPO service, anonymous |
| CCP_SPECIFIC | One named security | same | TCH REPO |
| TRIPARTY | Baskets GOVT, IG (corporate IG + AAA tranches), EQUITY (GX100) | Banks, funds, LIC4, MMFs as cash lenders | Tri-party agent = an NPC custodian bank; not CCP |
| BILATERAL | Anything eligible in §3.15.6 incl. HY, lower tranches, crypto majors (PB only) | PB clients, banks | Bilateral with CSA-like margining |
Central banks (04) run OMO repos and a standing repo facility through the same records.

#### §3.12.2 Hourly GC repo auction (per bloc, P8 at `t mod 30 == 29`)
Cash lenders submit (amount, minimum rate), cash borrowers (amount, maximum rate, collateral basket); uniform-price clearing at the rate where supply meets demand; ties prorated; remainders by hash. NPC reservation rates: MMFs lend at ≥ `DFR − 0.10%` (they have no CB access), banks with excess reserves lend at ≥ `DFR`, dealers borrow at ≤ `ONR + 0.10%`. The clearing rate is the **GC rate** (PUBLIC). Tenors in the auction: overnight (to next day close) and 1 game week; term repos (28, 91 game days) trade by RFQ (06 §3.19).

#### §3.12.3 Specials
`specificRate_i = GC − specialness_i`, `specialness_i = 0.02 · max(0, U_i − 0.60) / 0.40` (annual), where `U_i` is the securities-lending utilization of bond i (§3.13). Heavily shorted bonds can trade at negative repo rates (the cash lender pays to borrow the bond).

#### §3.12.4 Haircuts and margining
Haircut from §3.15.6 at the venue's stress multiplier. Collateral is marked every tick (06 marks); exposure checked each game hour: `required = cash · (1 + accrued) / (1 − h)`; if collateral value < required − MTA (`$10k` or 0.5% of cash), a margin call is issued, due in `PARAM.repo_call_due_ticks = 30`. Unmet → event of default: the non-defaulting party sells the collateral through the liquidation engine (§3.16.4) and claims any shortfall (unsecured claim; via the CCP for CCP venues, which then uses the REPO waterfall).
Substitution: the borrower may substitute collateral of equal or better value after haircut (`bank.repo.substitute`).

#### §3.12.5 Title transfer and re-use
Repo is a sale with an obligation to repurchase: legal title moves to the cash lender, which may re-use (re-pledge or sell) the collateral in BILATERAL and CCP venues (`reuseAllowed`), not in TRIPARTY. Accounting (03): the borrower keeps the security on its balance sheet as **encumbered** and books a repo liability; the lender books a receivable, not the security; if the lender re-uses or sells it, the lender books an obligation to return it. **Invariant (20):** every unit of every security has exactly one legal holder, and every chain of re-use claims on it is finite and acyclic and ends at that holder.

#### §3.12.6 Repo runs
Dealers' haircut stress multipliers (§3.15.6) rise with fear; when a borrower's PD rises (06), TRIPARTY and BILATERAL lenders add a counterparty add-on `+20% · PD/0.05` to haircuts and refuse rollovers above `PD > 0.10`. A fund or bank that relies on overnight repo for its assets can lose funding in a single day: funding liquidity is a distinct risk from market liquidity (V3 §3.4).
