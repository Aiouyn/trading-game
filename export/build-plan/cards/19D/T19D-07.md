## Goal
Make the production deployment of 19 Rule 3.13.4 reproducible and observable: alpha on one dedicated EU server plus object storage and CDN, beta and later on a container platform across three availability zones with a disaster-recovery copy in a second region; a CDN with WAF and volumetric protection in front of web, gateways and static assets, gateways accepting only CDN traffic, no public address for world hosts, databases and the bus (Rule 3.9.10, threat E14); optional edge gateways that terminate sockets near players and forward over the bus. Platform keys rotate every `platform_key_rotation_days` with the old key kept for verification (Rule 3.9.6). SLOs and burn-rate alerts of Rule 3.15.2, tracing sample of Rule 3.15.3, the integrity dashboard of Rule 3.15.4. Backups of Rule 3.7.7 and the chapter restore drill (19#48).

## Implementation steps
1. Topology (Rule 3.13.4): `ALPHA` and `BETA` are typed descriptions rendered into the platform's deployment manifests; gateways sized by `gatewaysFor` (at least 2, in at least two AZs); world hosts sized for the stage targets of Rule 3.13.1 (launch: one host with >= 32 cores plus standby; architecture target about 40 hosts). The cost benchmark of roadmap M34 is a documented step before choosing the beta platform.
2. Edge (Rule 3.9.10, 3.9.7): the CDN terminates TLS 1.3 with HSTS preload; WAF managed rules and per-IP rate limits on HTTP; WebSocket upgrades allowed only on `/v1/ws` (game) and `api.<domain>/v1/ws` (T19D-03); origins accept traffic only from CDN ranges (network policy plus `trustedProxy` in the gateway); world hosts, PostgreSQL, NATS, Valkey and ClickHouse have no public addresses (`infra/network/policies.ts`). Gateways shed new connections before existing ones above `gateway_max_sockets`. Edge gateways in other continents terminate sockets and forward over the bus; intake ordering still uses the coordinator's receive timestamp (§8 edge 1).
3. Keys (Rule 3.9.6): secrets in the managed secret store only; platform signing keys live in the KMS or HSM and are never exported; `rotateDue` flags any purpose whose ACTIVE key is older than 90 days; `rotate` creates the new version, marks the previous VERIFY_ONLY (kept for verifying old batches, commitments and reports), publishes the new public key to verifiers, writes an audit row. The world seed stays in the KMS-protected environment, readable only by world hosts.
4. Observability: alert rules for every metric of Rule 3.15.1 with an SLO; burn-rate pages per `burnRateAlerts`; `ticks_missed_total > 0` pages immediately; the tick trace is sampled 1 in `tick_trace_sample` (60) and 100% for late or aborted ticks (configured in T19B-03's tracer); logs carry no personal data. Dashboards: tick timing per phase and partition, and the integrity dashboard of Rule 3.15.4 (money minted vs destroyed per tick by reason, top 20 wealth deltas per game day with explanation, P2P flow graph, contract defaults, flagged accounts, tick timing) over T19C-09's projection; it never feeds the engine.
5. Backups (Rule 3.7.7): PostgreSQL WAL archiving with PITR for `pg_pitr_days` (35) and a daily base backup; ClickHouse daily backups kept 30 days; object storage versioned and replicated to the DR region; database primary failure promotes the standby within 60 s and ticks wait meanwhile (§8 edge 21); two consecutive failed daily snapshots alert (§8 edge 22).
6. Restore drill (every chapter, 8 real weeks): restore staging PostgreSQL to a point from production backups, copy the snapshots and batch archive, replay one game day (720 ticks) from the restored state with `replayHeadless` and compare each TickRecord hash with production; result recorded and alerted on failure.
