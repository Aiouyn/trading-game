## Goal
Turn the clearing price into exact whole-lot allocations and counterparty pairs: ration the side with excess interest by price, time, displayed-before-hidden, players-before-maker and pro rata with deterministic remainders; pair fills by a two-pointer walk; prevent self-trades within a beneficial-owner group by decrementing both sides and re-running; enforce FOK; cap at 3 passes. Output the `AuctionResult`, per-participant fills and the maker's net fill `q_N` used by T05-04.

## Implementation steps
1. After `choosePrice` (T05-05), at `p*` the side with excess interest is rationed and the other side fills fully (05 §3.5.3). Marketable buys: limit ≥ p* or MARKET; marketable sells: limit ≤ p* or MARKET. The maker's quantity at p* on each side is `makerBidQtyAtPrice(p*)` / `makerAskQtyAtPrice(p*)`.
2. Priority on the rationed side, keys in order:
   1. price (better limit first; MARKET best);
   2. `priorityTick` ascending (new orders this tick have N);
   3. displayed quantity before hidden reserve: model each iceberg as two virtual entries, displayed (its priorityTick) and hidden (priorityTick = N, it refreshes the display only after the displayed slice fills; 05 §3.5.3 item 3);
   4. at the same limit price level, the maker's ladder quantity after every resting order and pool at that level (D078, "players before the maker"); within the maker's cumulative quantity the inner levels are better prices, so the maker is split by level: maker quantity strictly better than p* is allocated with its price priority, maker quantity exactly at p* ranks last;
   5. equal keys: pro rata by remaining quantity in whole lots (each gets `floor(available × remaining_i / Σ remaining)` lots; do not use core `allocate`, whose largest-remainder rule differs), leftover lots one at a time in ascending order of a u53 draw per tied participant from `mkt.lp_quote:<assetId>` (the registered stream that replaces 05's `mkt/alloc`; one draw per tied participant, participants in canonical key order before drawing, so the draw count is fixed by the tie set).
3. Pairing (05 §3.5.4): sort filled buys and filled sells by their allocation priority (maker and pools inserted at their priority positions), two-pointer walk producing `Pair`s. Fill price is `p*` for every pair (INV-MKT-001).
4. STP: if a pair has the same `ownerGroupId` on both sides (15's beneficial-owner group via `groupOf`), decrement **both** orders by the paired quantity (cancel that quantity with reason `STP`, displayed first then hidden, 05 §8 edge 7), remove it, and re-run the whole auction (price selection included) without it. Pairs between different but linked groups (`linked`) are allowed and recorded in `linkedMatches` for surveillance F3 (D079; INV-MKT-002 forbids only same-owner fills).
5. FOK: after a pass, every FOK participant that would fill partially is removed (cancel reason `FOK_UNFILLED`) and the auction re-runs (edge 8: an FOK larger than all available interest is removed in pass 2, no partial).
6. Passes: at most `PARAM.mkt_max_auction_passes`. If STP or FOK conflicts remain after the last pass, remove the remaining conflicting quantities and recompute fills at the last pass's price without re-pricing (05 §3.5.4).
7. After the auction (05 §3.5.5): `makerNetFill` = maker sell qty − maker buy qty at p* (lots); per-class net taker quantity: each non-maker participant class (`PLAYERS` = ORDER participants of the player economy incl. AIs and operators; pool kinds; EXTERNAL mapped to its pool kind CB/TSY/LIQUIDATION/MANDATE) gets its signed net quantity taken from the maker, computed by pro-rating the maker's fills over the pairs that touch the maker. `aggressorSide` = BUY if makerNetFill > 0, SELL if < 0, NONE if 0. `imbalance` from T05-05 at p*.
8. Edge 16 (two whales on opposite sides): they pair with each other first because the maker ranks after them at equal price; the maker only takes the net residual, so `makerNetFill` (and hence the tally) is the residual only.
9. Exploit #6 (latency races): impossible because all of the tick's participants are one sealed set; nothing here uses arrival order.
